## What a Small World

**Format:** Letter

An improbable chain of connection that spans continents: at an origami convention in the UK, a chance meeting with a Chinese doctor leads back to Dr. Parchure and Vatsala Akka, one of RV's most beloved housemothers, and further back to Brockwood Park. A meditation on how RV alumni have scattered across the world, each carrying the school's spirit of mentorship and kindness.

<!-- sourceNote: wanderer, RVS Alumni WhatsApp group, 13 September 2025, 2:32 am IST (private record; published under an Anonymous profile per site policy) -->

Here's a funny story to share with you all, but not really funny. More like amazing. And very much a Rishi Valley story. How our school is so connected around the world, and literally every time I engage with someone new and randomly, I find a connection with Rishi Valley.

I fold paper. It's one of my hobbies. I have been folding paper for more than 30 years. And right now I am attending an origami convention in the UK. The convention is just starting this evening, Friday evening, and it'll go on through the weekend, Saturday and Sunday.

After dinner, I was walking back to my dorm room; we are all staying in dorms because the convention is being held at a university. The students aren't here yet, so we get to use the classrooms and dorm rooms for our conference. As I was walking back to the dorm room, I met a man, a Chinese man, who I didn't know, but I recognized him because he's in the same dorm as me and he had some trouble getting into his room earlier in the day and I had helped him, but we hadn't really chatted then and I didn't know anything about him.

And so I asked him where he's from, and he said he's from Oxford. He asked me where I'm from and I said, "From India," to which he asked, "Where in India?" Now when people ask that question, you never really know if they know a lot about India, but anyway I try to explain, so I said, "Do you know where Mumbai is?" I said, "Well, I'm living in a town 4 hours inland from Mumbai. It's called Pune." And to my surprise, he said, "Oh, I know Pune. I've been there many times. I used to travel there 3-4 times a year." Now this is unusual, unless you know people there or have business there or come for yoga or meditation or Osho or whatever, but that wasn't the case with him. He said to me, "I used to visit a friend of mine who lived there, and he was like a father to me," so I said, oh wow, this is interesting. He said, "When I came to the UK in the 70s I didn't know anybody, and this doctor, he was a doctor, this doctor helped me and he mentored me and he was like a father to me, and so when he moved back to India, he was in Pune and I would go visit him," and so just to make conversation I asked him what was the name of the doctor, and it was very windy outside and I couldn't really hear what he said, but it sounded like he said Dr. Parchoor. I don't know exactly how he said it, but it didn't sound like how we say it. But then I said, "Oh, Dr. Parchure?" OK, and just by chance I asked him what the Dr.'s wife's name was, and I didn't even know if the Dr. had a wife or not. I just asked the question, and he said, "Her name is Vatsala!" Whaaat! I almost lost it, I mean, what are the chances of this connection?

Anyway, long story short, and I'm sure many of you know who Dr. Parchure was and who Vatsala Parchure is: she is Parchure akka to us. My first year in RV, I was eight years old, and I was in blue house or red house, and she was house mother then, strict but sweet. I think I was a little mischievous then!

Later on, her son Vikram Sir also was at Rishi Valley with his family, Ambika akka and Pragnya, and Vishwas sir was also there. The guy I met, he's a doctor. He's a chiropractor and he's a folder, that's how I met him. He considered Dr. Parchure a second father to him, and he knows the whole family, and he knows the sons, and he knows about Rishi Valley, so I said, "Parchure akka was my teacher," and he said he knew of Rishi Valley, and then it turned out that in the late 70s, when he had come to the UK, it was to go to Brockwood Park, and he said that he worked there as the archivist.

What a small world. I would not have thought of this kind of connection when I started talking to him.

These kinds of stories are more frequent, and I really shouldn't be surprised anymore, but I still am, because they're so beautiful, and it makes me realize more and more that we are very lucky to have been at Rishi Valley, not just for the beautiful campus, but for all the people who studied there and all the people who taught us, and everyone has now gone out into the world and continued that spirit of Rishi Valley wherever they are, by mentoring people, helping people, changing lives, etc.

Anyway, just wanted to share this with you all, thank you.

## A story about RV. And Nicobar.

**Format:** Letter

A serendipitous encounter in a Bangalore design studio links a tsunami survivor from the Nicobar Islands—supported by Rahul Bose's foundation—back to her transformative years at RV and her onwards journey into design school. A story of how far a school's ripples travel.

<!-- sourceNote: Chatura, RVS Alumni WhatsApp group, 9 October 2025 (private record only; published anonymously; batchmate named in the original text redacted pending confirmed consent to be named. Unnamed Nicobar-tsunami student's description retained as positive and non-identifying per owner's call, with "Srishti school of design" softened to "a design school"; her name must never be published if it surfaces later.) -->

Some time ago, my batchmate and friend and I were at a store called Nicobar in Bangalore. The store spreads across 2 levels and we were on the top level browsing. There were two young women downstairs having a conversation and it sounded like they were prepping for a shoot/interview. We could hear them chatting, and then at one point we heard the interviewer say to the interviewee - "And then let's talk about your time in RV."

!!!

So we said - almost simultaneously and rather loudly and enthusiastically - "Hi! Were you in RV?"

It turns out the girl being interviewed was from the island of Nicobar (hence the location for the shoot). She had the opportunity to go to RV, thanks to Rahul Bose's foundation that supported her and some others after the deadly tsunami of 2004. After RV she got into a design school and was well on her way to making a career in design. And this was an interview about her journey and the opportunities that life had granted her, or she had made for herself.

Quite serendipitous and quite lovely.

## My Own Self-Created Mt Kailash

**Format:** Letter

A nail-biting prank in the senior hostel bathrooms—locking toilet stalls from the inside and escaping through a gap above the walls—spirals into a crisis when both a stranger and the housemother appear mid-escape. Decades later, the mystery visitor is revealed to be a friend's father, and the fear crystallizes into a cherished memory of a walk back through the dark valley. A masterwork of suspense and reflection.

<!-- sourceNote: Capt (Dr) Rohit Shetty, WhatsApp "RVS Alumni" group chat, 26 December 2025, 10:12 am IST (source: "/Users/sanan/Documents/rv-connect/WhatsApp/WhatsApp Chat - RVS Alumni/_chat.txt", lines 3534–3549). For the private record only; to be published under an Anonymous byline on the site per the owner's instruction. -->

That gap on top of the bathroom walls… I have a story :))

Someday, when all is forgotten about this alumni meet at RV, I shall write about it. But as of now, I have to, have to write about an incident from school, the memory of which has never left me, though it had dissipated to a hazy hue. But it all came back so, so vividly, when during our alumni meet this time, among other familiar faces that floated in from a distant past, I saw the friendly, but now frail face of Nivedita Akka, our house mother at Kailash house.

Of my four years at RV, three were spent in Neem B house. And the final one year was a big move. To the senior boys hostel. To Kailash house.

Now, let me get a confession out of the way (though the Sarahah wave is long past). If any of you Kailash house boys of 10th (Chocka, Manish, Gm, Saran, Maha, Kumble, Dinakar) waited a while to use the toilets and then found no one inside though the door was locked from inside, that was my doing :)). The senior boys hostel was a big, bad place, far removed from the innocent Neem B house. There was the Dark Room game (already explained, what seems a long time ago) which I could not handle, and other pranks too, which I could. One such prank was to lock the toilets from outside, when someone was using them.

Sooner or later, I had to be a victim, and as expected, it happened. I was locked in once, and that's when I made a fantastic discovery. I don't know how many of you remember the washrooms' "architecture" of the boys hostel, but it was something like this. A set of six toilets, where none of the walls went all the way up to the ceiling. Which meant there was a gap between the walls and the ceiling, a God sent gap. I maneuvered myself up the wall, got into that space between the toilets, and jumped down the other side, and came out from the next toilet :)). This happened a few times and I always came out of the washroom area, whistling loudly, just to make sure that whoever locked me in, wondered how I got out.

And then, one fine day towards the latter half of 10th std, it struck me. How about a prank of my own? If I could get locked in, I could lock others "OUT" too! And that's how I ended up locking the doors from the inside, and then jump out of the next toilet!

I would do this occasionally. And one fine day my confidence peaked so high that I decided to lock five of the six toilets and leave one out (not out of humanitarian considerations but because I had to get out through the door of the sixth :)).

But I had to do it at a time when there was the least possibility of me being caught out. I chose the juice break time. During juice break, we would all come back to the hostel, have lemon juice and get back in a hurry to the classes. I waited until everyone had left, and then put my plan into action. Got into the first toilet, locked it from inside, climbed the wall, jumped over it onto the next toilet, locked it, went onto the next. Just when I was on to the wall of the fourth, the unthinkable. My eyes caught sight of a stranger, a man I had never seen before, walk into the washroom area. I watched disbelievingly, and in complete fear, as he walked from door to door, pushing and finding it locked. And then, he went into the bathrooms, opened the doors of each, looking in, and moving onto the next. I was numb. I sat crouched atop the wall, my breathing shallow and almost at a standstill, heartbeat loud, as the gentleman washed his face, brought out his handkerchief, and wiped his face, in a manner that seemed familiar. And that face... where had I seen him before? And then, he came towards the toilets again, and just as I hit panic mode, he turned away, and went out of the washroom area.

I breathed out. But my brain was in turmoil. Who was he? His face familiar, but I was sure I hadn't seen him ever before in RV. My hands and legs took a while to move again. I rested a few seconds, seconds that turned out crucial. For, just before I could jump down from the wall, it all got infinitely worse. In walked Nivedita Akka, our housemother. As I watched in horror, she went about inspecting the bathrooms, and then she slowly walked towards the washing stones, and looked at the happily drying "undies and bundies". Then she turned back towards the toilets, and incredibly, she inspected the one, only one toilet of the two that I hadn't yet locked from the inside. I froze. Dear-o-dear, maybe she was going to look up now, find me, and end my torture. I was going to be the talk of RV this evening, or latest by the next morning. Were the guys going to term me a pervert? A voyeur?

I just closed my eyes, and I prayed. When I opened my eyes, she had moved away from the toilets and was inspecting the wash basins. And then, she slowly walked out from there.

I did not waste a second this time. I mean, given the sloppy luck I was having that day in carrying out a simple prank, gone horribly wrong, I wouldn't be surprised if Thomas Akka were to walk in next. I jumped from the wall, and after a decent gap of a minute or two, rushed out from the washroom area, out of the Kailash house and towards the classrooms, all the while tense, and wondering, who the mystery man was. I thought I must tell the others about the incident, but the only thing stopping me, was, what if everyone asked what I was up to in the first place?

I kept the whole incident to myself during lunch, then till asthachal, then till prep. And just before dinner, sitting near the tree outside the DH, I decided that I was going to tell the others about this. And as the DH bell went off and I looked up, I saw dear Kumble. He said "Hi. Meet my dad." And as I looked at the senior Kumble next to him, my heart skipped a beat. For as I looked up, looking down at me with a friendly smile, was my mystery man! Our Kumble junior was a replica of him! And hence the familiar look and mannerisms!! He was, that morning, most likely checking out the hygiene at the house where his dear son was living.

Now Suresh Kumble's dad wasn't a frequent visitor to RV. In fact, in all four years at RV, that was his first and only visit, if I remember correctly. Those were days before the present "term visit" and "day visit" and "gate visit" and the special "shah/kamat" visits :))

Oh the relief I felt that moment... Kumble Uncle may have thought I was being overly dramatic in my expression of pleasure in having met him. Dinner that evening was savoured so well, unlike my tense lunch that afternoon.

That was my first and last attempt at a prank. But to this day, I do not know if Nivedita Akka saw me when I had my eyes closed tight. Is it that she just did not know how to react to what she saw, for she was new to this housemother thing too, or was her peripheral vision weak that she did not even glimpse me, or was her long term vision for a child of 14 so strong that she ignored what she saw?

I don't know. But I know one thing. In later years, I have climbed mountains to places of worship, I have climbed to Amarnath (where I have had another hilarious experience, but that's for another day and another time), I have trekked up to Vaishnodevi. But never have I prayed as fervently as I did, atop my own self-created Mt Kailash, all those years ago. And I also know, that to some philosophical questions like "What is fear?" that was so often asked at RV, the answers lay at the strangest of places, as strange as a gap between the walls and ceiling of washrooms of Kailash house :))

## That Beautiful Walk in the Darkness

**Format:** Letter

Five roommates leave their ICSE exam prep a day before their final papers to accompany a friend to the hospital at midnight, searching for a doctor to remove a red ant from his ear. The walk back through the dark, eerie landscape becomes a crystalline moment of friendship and impending separation, reminding him that fear dissolves in the presence of those who care.

<!-- sourceNote: Capt (Dr) Rohit Shetty, 29 May 2026 (WhatsApp Chat - RVS Alumni, lines 7527–7537) -->

I have a story about my roomies from 10th std.

Six of us (no selfies back then, so someone was always missing, and don't ask why one or two of us were always shirtless) shared a room during the second half of tenth, in the midst of all those crazy "series" (isn't that what we called those mock exams?), and the 10th final ICSE exams.

So, we were in the midst of those final exams (a day before the history/geography/civics exams, to be precise), immersed in those books, and it was past midnight. And in that quietness, out of the blue, I found some strange sensation in my ear and before I knew it, some insect had parked itself inside my ear. I tried everything to get it out. Flushing water into my ear, Chocka trying his earbuds, everything. And then it started to hurt. Try as I might, nothing worked. There was only one thing left to do. Expert help, from the Doc couple.

But one minor problem. I don't know if you all remember RV in the night time. It's very quiet, very dark, and for me, very scary out in the open. Added to that was the fact that a few days earlier, we had found large patches of bloodstains outside a teacher's room, on the other side, in Kailash house. And rumours of hearing the net doors of the common room open and shut during the dead of night didn't make it a comfortable thought to walk up to the doc's house.

But then, the irritating earache. After informing Nivedita akka, our housemother (yes, she was our housemother in, of all places, senior boys' hostel!), I decided to go. All the other five promptly closed their books and said, "Let's go." (This was final exams, remember.)

And we walked in the dead of night. Out of the house, in the eerie silence, across the two football fields, past the throwball courts, past DH, to the hospital building. We had to knock on the doors and windows too, to wake up the doc. And he came out, set up all his paraphernalia, and flushed out what looked like a red ant from my ear. And I think it was Rahul who kept asking, "Are you sure it's out? Are you sure? We don't want to walk back here in the dark."

And then we walked back to the boys' hostel. And this time as we walked back, past the DH, past the throwball fields, down into the football fields, I was strangely happy even through the imaginary rustles that we heard through the bushes, and the real howl of the foxes (or was it wolves) we heard from the mountains above. The Cave Rock hill on our right formed a beautiful silhouette even in that darkness. Added to that, the cool wind, well, that walk shall always remain unforgettable to me.

Did I say I felt happy? Correction. I felt a bit sad too. It kind of hit me at that moment (and for the first time) that in a few more days, all this would be gone (I already knew I was going to leave RV after 10th.) This place I did not like so much, these friends that I really liked, everything.

I do remember we chatted about inane things on the way back, as to which questions were expected in the next day's paper, was it going to be Ashoka or Chandragupta Maurya, was it going to be the Prairies or the Grasslands or the plains or the Andes or the Alps, etc. But I only remember one thing. That these five roomies of mine left their studies, a day before final exams, to take me to the doc, even though they were, as they confessed to Nivedita akka the next day, "shit" scared of that darkness.

That beautiful walk in the darkness has always been one of my pleasant memories of RV, though I do keep wondering about those blotches of bloodstains right outside a teacher's door.

Who played that prank, I always wonder.

## June 12, 1966

**Format:** Post

Sathya Gourisankar marks the sixtieth anniversary of his arrival at RV as a homesick ten-year-old. His first English class under Mark Lee sir, who noticed his tears and showed him kindness that blossomed into a lifelong friendship and mentorship. A poignant snapshot of how small gestures of care at RV rippled across decades.

<!-- sourceNote: SATHYA GOURISANKAR, 12/06/2026 (June 12, 2026), 9:07:10 pm — WhatsApp Chat - RVS Alumni/_chat.txt, line 8005 -->

Today, June 12th, happens to be the exact date sixty years ago (i.e., June 12, 1966) when my parents took me and my brother to RV on a Sunday morning to join us there. We were given a room in the old guest house then (K's building, below his dwelling floor).

I am nostalgically recollecting my first impressions of the school as I felt it as a ten-year-old kid, coming out of the comfort of my home in Chittoor (where also I had a pampered life) and recollect that Sunday lunch in RV those days when there were two items that remained steady for many years - Curd rice with mango pickle and Semiya Payasam!

My first day of class in RV was the next day, Monday, June 13, 1966, in J-4, in the room next to the Junior school tree. My first period was a two-period class in English by Mark Lee sir. I recollect as I sat in his class, I was sobbing feeling homesick since my parents had just bid goodbye and I was constantly looking out the window to see if I could spot them driving out on the road! After a while, Mark Lee sir noticed my discomfort and asked a classmate to help me and took special care to put me at ease and make me comfortable. Little did I know then that Mark Lee sir was destined to become my dear friend-cum-mentor with whom I shared a close relationship later in life, for many years after I left RV!

Sorry for this outpouring of RV nostalgia. Today just happens to be the sixtieth anniversary of my setting foot in RV, so very special to me!

## The Hippy Rebellion

**Format:** Post

In 1969, six students scandalously shaved their heads entirely, a gesture of rebellion during the hippy movement. Principal Dr. B flew into a rage, yet within days showed his true nature by writing a letter to the offender's father asking for leniency and a homecoming, revealing the compassionate discipline beneath his stern exterior. A story about a principal who punished with reformative intent.

<!-- sourceNote: Sathya Gourisankar, 29 November 2025 (RVS Alumni WhatsApp group, line 1893; kept for the private record only, published on the site as Anonymous). -->

One more "Chinna Katha" to post from my memory bank before time and age rob me of my memories! It must have been 1969 when this happened. Those were days of the hippy movement, boys were fond of having long hair. The school barber from Thettu would come to each hostel on Sundays by turns to give haircuts to students pre-chosen by the house masters.

On one such occasion, in the senior year, a bunch of about six students decided to show their rebellion by having their hair totally tonsured (bodi in Telugu). The barber, whose face I still recollect, was a nice, obliging man who would do anything we asked for! Dr. Balasundaram, the Principal, was a stern man those days but also had another side to him which I will mention later. When he learnt of this incident, he flew into a rage and yelled at the students, with one part of the yelling I recollect being, "Fellows, know that this is not Tirupati for doing such things!"

I will not mention the names. Of the six, one was from Calcutta, one from Hyderabad, one from Bangalore that I remember; others I cannot recollect. Dr. B decided to "punish" in his style. The fellow closest from Bangalore was packed in the school car and sent to his home.

Now, I will narrate what Dr. B himself told some of us a few days after this incident. He said after sending that boy home, he felt so sad and could not sleep that night. At around 2 am the following morning, he sat up and wrote a letter to the boy's father asking him to give a good time to the boy, keep him home for two days and send him back to school.

Now comes another input on this matter decades later when I met Mark Lee sir at his home in Ojai. We were chatting about old RV incidents in our period there as always we did and ended up talking about this with him. He said, "Sathya, you do not know of a sequel to that incident which I shall tell you about. Krishnaji visited RV a few months after this incident happened. He was given an update on the incident. When he heard of it, it seems Krishnaji scolded Dr. B saying, 'Balu, why did you let these chaps off the hook so easily? You should have been more strict with them.'" Mark Lee sir told me there was a part of K that was not known to many where he would demand discipline!

To me, it is yet another tale of an RV teacher relating to and treating students as their own family by opting for reformative punishment rather than retributive punishment! Dr. B was a strange mix of sternness and compassion!

## The Banyan Tree

**Format:** Letter

The majestic Banyan tree at RV's heart, planted beside which the school itself was born. From childhood terror of walking to it alone at night, to adolescent romance, to its decline and conservation in 2002 when the stage was removed to save it. A meditation on how a single tree holds generations of memory, fear, and belonging.

<!-- sourceNote: B R Venkatesh, 22 November 2025 (WhatsApp Chat - RVS Alumni, lines 1293–1305, for the private record only; publishing under Anonymous profile) -->

When Krishnaji (Jiddu Krishnamurti) was scouting to start a school near his home town of Madanapalle, he came to the Valley and was mesmerised by the beauty of this Valley, what with all the hills which are supposed to be older than the Mighty Himalayas, and in the centre of it was this Huge Banyan Tree. Something happened, and he wanted this place to start the school, and hence was born THE RISHI VALLEY SCHOOL.

The cherry on top of the pie was this majestic and Mighty tree. A stage was built with some stone benches like an amphitheater.

Our love for this iconic tree grew with each passing year. Initially, it was fearful for the small kid that I was, who was afraid to go alone, especially at night (when there was a dance recital or a play that was staged there). With a prayer on my lips and a Jeep torch that would hardly light the path to the tree, I managed to reach the destination. During the entire show, I would be on the lookout for reptiles and snakes. Fortunately, there was never an untoward incident that I know.

As I grew older, I was less afraid and would walk past that tree as if everything was fine. It was also one of the hot spots for young couples to meet and learn about the birds and bees.

In December 2002, our batch met in Rishi Valley to celebrate our 25th anniversary. The stage was removed as it was choking the tree, and conservation was done to protect the tree.

## A Creative Artist

**Format:** Letter

Sivarajan, a shy and reclusive music teacher who played sitar, sang ghazals, and painted—but only in the shelter of his room, stopping abruptly if he sensed an audience. A portrait of an isolated creative genius who loved children but withdrew from adults, his extraordinary talents never shared with the world outside his inner solitude.

<!-- sourceNote: Harshad Parekh, 22 August 2025 -->

I had good contact with Sivarajan in Rishi Valley for many years. We met on a road accidentally, between the dining hall and the old guest house, many times, and we talked about music and art for a long time while standing on the road.

He was a good singer, sitar player and a painter. But he never sang or played the sitar for anyone. Nor did he show his paintings to anyone.

Once I took him up on the meditation hill above the Asthachal hill, my favourite place. I requested him to sing for me. He was in a good mood. He sang a ghazal, "Lagata Nahi Hai Dil Mera," a ghazal attributed to the last Mughal king, Bahadur Shah Zafar. He sang well.

He was very shy of people and suspicious too, with some strange superstitions. He played the sitar in his room, but if he came to know that someone was outside his room listening to the sitar, he would immediately stop it.

If he were smart, he could have earned lots of money by his talents. But he remained isolated in his inner world, which was not happy.

He was a creative artist. He loved little children. But he did not like to talk with adults.

He had lived in Shantiniketan for many years before he joined Rishi Valley School in the late 1980s.
