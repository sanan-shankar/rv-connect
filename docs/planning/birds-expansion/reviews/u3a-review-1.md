# U3a, independent review 1 (2026-10-15)

Review 1 of U3a ("Hard redraws"): the four birds redrawn from scratch, judged against `reviewer.md` by an Opus reviewer who did not draw them.

None pass. 35 Jacobin Cuckoo is one fault from passing: a sharp corner at the bottom of its white belly. 26 Paradise Flycatcher fails on its tail. 32 Peregrine Falcon reads as a penguin. 36 Black Eagle has the wrong eye colour and no eagle shape. One move recurs: the pale region on 32, 35 and 36 is built from the same template, and on all three its lower-left join turns 63 to 67 degrees. U2b review 2 failed a 65-degree join, so all three fail here too.

Inputs: the 600px renders, the 220/96/40px sheets in `e2e/.shots/birds/`, two extra look-alike sheets rendered for this review (`sheet-36_16_17_18_28_35.png`: the Eagle beside the Koel, Drongo, Coucal, Cormorant and Jacobin; `sheet-26_30_46_49_32_40_42.png`: the Flycatcher and Falcon beside the Egret, Munia, White-eye, Kite and Shrike, each with its 40px twin), the `draw()` bodies in `bird-avatar-v2.tsx`, and `bird-edges.mjs`. Every chord the edge audit lists (26: two, 35: two, 36: one) is under another shape, so none of them is a fault. Coordinates are viewBox units (0..100) from the source. "px" is a point on that bird's 600px render, after the re-centring in `bird-adjust.json`. A "turn" is the change of direction where two path segments join.

BIRD 26 Paradise Flycatcher: FAIL
Verdict: The head is right: glossy black crest, blue eye-ring, blue bill. But the long tail is the whole point of this bird (¶16), and it curls under the body and finishes in front of the breast, so it reads as a grey saucer the bird sits in, not a ribbon trailing behind it. Its tip is notched, and the white back has no edge on the paper at 40px.
Neighbours at 40px: Cattle Egret (30), Tricolored Munia (46), Jacobin Cuckoo (35), told apart by colour: a black head on a white body. The Egret has no black, the Munia's body is chestnut and the Jacobin's body is black.
Faults:
  1. [line 5] The tail does not read as a tail. The ribbon leaves the body at about (30,70), passes under the whole bird along y 85 to 93 from x 14 to x 76, and ends at (75,80.5). The body's front is at x 69, so the ribbon ends ahead of the breast, under the bill. A tail cannot end in front of the bird. At 600px (px (65,380) round to (516,455)) and on the 220 sheet it reads as a grey dish or hammock under a white ball. It is also #BFC1B7, a mid grey well apart from the body's white, which makes it look like a separate object rather than the bird's own white plumage. Fix: let the ribbon leave the vent and fall down and BACK, so it ends behind and below the bird and never crosses to the right of the body's centre (x about 45). For example, one loose S from about (36,76) down-left to a round end near (14,90). A perched male's streamers hang down from the vent, and that is the pose to draw. If the ribbon cannot get enough length inside r~45, make the bird smaller (body r about 19, head r about 12) and lift it to the upper right; the centroid script rescales. Bring the ribbon's colour nearer the body's shadow tone (SHD #D6D8CE), so it reads as white plumage in shade. If that loses it at 40px, keep one step darker, but no further.
  2. [line 1, line 2] The tail's tip is a notch, not a round end. `A1.8 1.8 0 0 1 73.9 79.1` has sweep flag 1, so the arc bulges back INTO the ribbon (its midpoint (73.6,81.6) is inside the band). That leaves two sharp corners at (76.1,81.9) and (73.9,79.1), px about (516,455). Fix: sweep flag 0, so the cap bulges forward. If fault 1 replaces the path, check the new end cap the same way.
  3. [line 8] The white body has no edge along its back. From about (23,50) over the back to (41,35), px (128,228) to (263,115), the cool white #ECEEE6 sits straight on the paper #F5F2EA. Nothing grey or dark runs along that rim: the wing lobe sits 8 units inside it (its edge at x 29.5 at y 59, against the rim at x 21). At 40px that quarter of the body vanishes, and the bird reads as a black head over a grey arc. This is the doubt the drawer flagged, and it is real. Fix: take the folded wing out to the rim, so its outer edge IS the body's edge from about (22,52) down to (33,78), the way the Cattle Egret's grey wing does. Or draw the male's real black primary edging along that rim; it is a true field mark and gives the white an edge.
Notes (not blocking):
  - The crest at (45-57, 21-35) is a tall lobe with a 6-unit round top that stands up off the crown. At 600px it reads a little like a single round ear. The real crest is a short tuft swept back from the nape, so lean it further back and lower.

BIRD 32 Peregrine Falcon: FAIL
Verdict: Far simpler than the old one, and a birder's marks are all present: dark helmet, moustache, yellow eye-ring and cere, slate back, pale barred front. But the wing hangs straight down the flank like a flipper, there is no tail, and the white front runs to the vent, so at 220 and 600px it reads as a penguin wearing a monocle, not a falcon. The eye-ring also sits on the cheek's edge, and three joins are sharp corners.
Neighbours at 40px: Jacobin Cuckoo (35), Magpie-Robin (14), Brahminy Kite (40), told apart by colour: the only slate-blue body with a cream front. The pied birds are black, the Kite is terracotta.
Faults:
  1. [line 5] The silhouette says penguin. The wing runs from the shoulder (49,41) almost straight down the left flank to a tip at (20-24.5, 83-86), past the rim at px about (105-125, 530), with nothing below it. Together with the cream front that fills the right half down to (42,84), that is a penguin's flipper and white shirt. On a perched falcon the folded wing lies along the back, and its tip crosses over a tail that shows below it. Fix: add a short slate tail below the rim at the lower left, a round-ended paddle like the Jacobin's or the Eagle's, about (40,72) to (22,90). Lay the wing's tip on top of it, pointing down and back rather than straight down. Fault 4 pays for the extra shape.
  2. [line 1] Three joins are sharp corners. (a) The wing tip's inner side at (24.5,86), on the paper: the edge arriving from Q21 87.5 heads right, the edge leaving towards Q26 73 heads nearly straight up, a turn of about 60 degrees, px about (122,532). (b) The same edge at (39,61), on the slate between wing and cream: a turn of about 37 degrees, px about (233,341). (c) The cream front's bottom-left at (42.2,84.1): about 63 degrees, px about (258,517). Fix: keep the control points either side of each join on one line. Wing, for example: `... Q18.5 67 20 83 Q21 88 24 86.8 Q27 85 28.5 76 Q31 64 40 56 Q45 49 49 41 Z` (joins turn 14 degrees or less). Cream, for example: `... Q56.3 86.6 44.5 84 Q40 83 39.8 74 Q40.5 64 46 58 Q49.2 53.2 49 44 Z` (joins turn 10 degrees or less, slate rim still about 2.9). If fault 1 redraws the wing, the new wing must meet the same rule.
  3. [line 4, line 8] The eye-ring sits on the cheek's edge. The ring (64,37) r 5 bottoms at y 42, and the cream's top edge passes y 41.3 at x 64, so the ring's lower arc lies on the cream. Yellow on cream is the weakest pair on the face. The moustache's top (60.5,41) is also 0.3 from the ring. Three edges meet in about one unit under the eye, px about (405-425, 190-197), so the face reads less cleanly than it should. Fix: lower the cream's top edge under the eye by about 2.5 units, e.g. `M49 46 Q58 42 67.5 45.5 Q71 48 73.9 52 ...`. That leaves at least 1.5 units of helmet all round the ring, and the moustache can start inside that band at about (60.5,43.5).
  4. [line 3] Too many marks. Beyond the body, helmet, wing, bill and eye there are four marks: the cream front, two bars and the moustache. With the cere and eye-ring the bird has twelve drawn shapes, and fault 1 has to add a tail. The bars at (60,66) and (58,73.5), px about (393,379) and (381,424), read as two floating grey pills at 600px and vanish at 96 and 40px. Fix: delete both. The helmet, moustache and yellow ring carry the falcon.
Notes (not blocking):
  - ¶22 asks for "the sheer strength of the bird" balanced with the cute. Beyond the hook, nothing says strength yet. A flatter crown on the helmet, as on a real falcon's head, would add it without a brow.

BIRD 35 Jacobin Cuckoo: FAIL
Verdict: A black crested cuckoo with its white underparts kept inside a black rim (his "white as white space", ¶25, solved), and the white wing flash on a darker folded wing. It reads as the bird, and at 40px apart from the Magpie-Robin by its crest and dropped tail. The one fault is a sharp corner at the bottom of the white belly.
Neighbours at 40px: Magpie-Robin (14), Tricolored Munia (46), Black Eagle (36). Told apart from 14 by silhouette: a crest bump and a tail dropping down-left, against a tail cocked up-left with no crest. From 46 by colour: the Munia has a chestnut disc. From 36 by colour: a white belly against an all-dark body.
Faults:
  1. [line 1] The white belly's bottom-left at (44.8,82.4) is a corner. The edge arriving from Q58 83.6 heads left, the edge leaving towards Q40 70 heads up, a turn of about 64 degrees, px about (283,507). U2b review 2 failed a 65-degree join. Fix: round it with collinear controls, e.g. replace `Q58 83.6 44.8 82.4 Q40 70 47 60` with `Q58 83.6 47 82.6 Q42 82 41.8 74 Q42 67 47 60` (joins turn 15 degrees or less; black rim about 3.4 below, about 3.5 clear of the wing lobe).
Notes (not blocking):
  - The wing lobe and the white lozenge are the Magpie-Robin's own: the same lobe path and a lozenge at -35 degrees in the same place, also reused for the Flycatcher's wing. At 220px the two pied birds read as siblings, and only the crest and tail separate them. A real Jacobin's flash is a short white bar across the base of the primaries, lower on the wing. Moving the lozenge there, as a bar across the lobe's lower third, would be truer and would tell the two apart more.
  - The crest's round top (46-58.5, 21-33.5) stands tall and can read for a moment as an ear at 600px. A little more lean back would read more as a crest.

BIRD 36 Black Eagle: FAIL
Verdict: Simpler, as asked: no brow, no brown spot, no scallops. But the eye is the wrong colour for this eagle, and nothing in the shape says eagle. It is a round dark ball with a paler breast, so at 96 and 40px it reads as a dark blackbird or myna with two yellow dots on its face. That answers the drawer's doubt: it does not read as an eagle.
Neighbours at 40px: Koel (16), Coucal (18), Drongo (17), told apart by colour: warm sooty brown with a yellow face, against cool blue-blacks with red eyes and a chestnut or blue wing. So line 6 holds within the set, even though nothing at 40px says eagle.
Faults:
  1. [line 5] The yellow iris is not the bird. The adult Black Eagle's iris is dark brown (HBW: dark brown at every age; BirdForum: brown). Only the cere and the feet are yellow. The drawing has a yellow iris #E9C043 r 4.2 at (63,34.5), and beside it, at the same height, a yellow cere #E7B833 of almost the same hue and size at (73.5,35.5). The face becomes two yellow discs in a row, px about (414,134) and (495,141), which at 96 and 40px read as two eyes or as the "spot on top of the beak" of ¶26 again. Fix: the house `Eye` with a disc darker than SOOT (e.g. #141110, r 4.4) and its catchlight. If it needs more presence on the sooty head, add a thin dark-brown iris ring (about #5E4430). The yellow then lives in the cere alone, as the one accent. Runner: the F22 row says "yellow cere and eye"; that is the action column, not his words. ¶26 says nothing about the eye's colour, and A7 asks for trueness, so this is yours to confirm.
  2. [line 5] Nothing in the shape says eagle. The only marking is the paler breast LIT #5C4E40 at (44-76, 48-85), which turns a dark round bird into a thrush with a brown front. On a real Black Eagle the paler part is the upperwing (BirdForum: paler secondaries and scapulars), and its long folded wings reaching the end of the tail are its perched outline. Fix: move the lit region from the breast to one long folded wing, from the shoulder under the head (about (52,44)) down the back to a round tip lying over the tail, past the rim at the lower left (as the Peregrine's wing trails). Put the breast back to SOOT. The shape count does not change.
  3. [line 1] The lit breast's bottom-left at (44.5,84.5) is a corner: about 67 degrees, px about (271,519). Fix, if the breast stays: `Q57.5 85.5 47 84.6 Q42 84 42 75 Q42.5 66 49 59` (joins turn 16 degrees or less, rim unchanged at about 2.4). If fault 2 moves the lit region to a wing, the new wing must have no join turning more than about 20 degrees.
Notes (not blocking):
  - The cere is a 7.2 by 6.2 ellipse laid OVER the bill's base. It bulges into the face, so at 600px it reads as a yellow ball with a grey hook coming out of it. A real cere is the yellow base of the upper bill. Drawn as a saddle that follows the bill's top edge, about half the eye's height, it would read as the bill's own yellow base, and nobody could take it for a "spot on top of the beak" (¶26).

Sources for the Black Eagle's eye: [HBW, Indian Black Eagle](https://www.hbw.com/species/indian-black-eagle-ictinaetus-malayensis), [BirdForum Opus, Black Eagle](https://birdforum.net/opus/Black_Eagle), [Wikipedia, Black eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_eagle).
