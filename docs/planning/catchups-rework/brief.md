# The owner's brief, in his own words

**Dates: 2026-09-04 and 2026-09-05. Spoken, transcribed, in two sittings; then typed
follow-ups on 2026-09-05 while the campaign was being set up.** This file is the source of
truth for what was asked for. Every session working this campaign reads THIS file, in full,
first. The handover indexes it; nothing replaces it.

**What was cleaned, and what was not.** Fillers ("um", "uh", "like", "you know", "okay" as a
tic) are removed. Stutters and restarts are merged ("It's, it's" reads "it's"). The
transcription's "ketchup" is written "catch-up" throughout. Where a word is unclear or a
mis-hearing is suspected, **the word as transcribed stays in the sentence** and the guess sits
beside it in square brackets; the earlier version of this file did the reverse, and it was
changed after the check described below. Words that name a button or a section on screen are
capitalised (View, Start one, Fresh off the press) so they read as labels; that is typography,
not content. Numbers are written as he said them. The spoken parts use British spelling,
because a transcript has no spelling of its own; the typed parts (¶48 onward) keep his exact
spelling and typos. Everything else is his: same content, same order, same emphasis, every
reversal, every hedge ("I think", "I guess", "maybe", "I don't know", "a little bit", "kind
of", "or something"), every "right?", every swear word, because the register is part of the
instruction. He asked for exactly this treatment in ¶22:

> "Maybe you can delete the uhs and the ahs and the other grammatical quirks, but I'd like
> the main content and nuance to be there."

And he explained why a summary would not do:

> "When you do this summarisation, that's where you get the lower quality of output, because
> these subagents or future agents are not able to capture the nuance in what I'm saying.
> They have to work off this reductionist view of the previous agent's judgment of what they
> thought was important and what I've said, and then I don't get exactly what I want."

**Checked against the raw transcript.** On 2026-09-05 four independent readers compared each
stretch of the raw transcript against this file, sentence by sentence, looking for anything
dropped, hardened, reordered or silently corrected. They found 69 things, most of them hedges
and question tags the first cut had trimmed, one whole sentence of opinion, three
self-corrections he made out loud, and about a dozen unbracketed word changes. All are folded
in below. The raw transcript itself is not on disk; it lives in the set-up session's
conversation, and the four readers' findings are in that session's workflow journal.

Paragraph numbers in **[n]** are the only structural addition. They exist so that
[`handover.md`](handover.md) can point into this file. Anything in square brackets is the
transcriber's note and nothing else is.

Two things he refers to by name: **"In the loop"** is a live Catch-up he runs, and **"Test"**
is an ended one. **Mohini** and **Cyan** are real members whose answers he was reading.
