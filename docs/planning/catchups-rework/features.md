### The one rule that came out of this and touches a page already drawn

**You cannot read the answers until you have written your own — but once the Edition is published,
it is open to everybody whether they wrote or not.**

That is a sharper rule than either half on its own. While an Edition is being written, reading is
earned. Once it is out, it is a record and a record is not a reward. It also means somebody who
joins the batch in 2027 can read every earlier Edition, which is ¶4 and already true.

**It changes the answering surface, which is build phase 7**, and the reader, which is phase 8. It
is not a new table: an Edition is `collecting`, `answering` or `published` already, and "have I
answered" is one row. Whoever takes phase 7 needs this before they draw the page.

### F3, then and now, said plainly

You are asked one question. You answer it with **two photographs instead of words**: one from when
you were at school, one from now. The Edition prints them side by side, yours next to everyone
else's.

"The dorm then, the dorm now." "Me at fifteen, me at sixty." "The banyan in 1978, the banyan last
month."

The reason it was on the list is that no other product in this space could do it: it only works if
there is a pile of old photographs of the same place, and the Valley Collection is exactly that.
The reason it may be a bad idea is that half the batch will not have a photograph from school on
their phone, and a question most people cannot answer is a hole in the page.

**One sentence from you settles it.**

## 2. The question library, which is what you asked for advice on

You asked three things: whether to use a library at all, what kinds of questions to put in it, and
how to do it without the library becoming enormous.

### Use one, but never show all of it

Letterloop's 600 is not a feature, it is a filing problem. Picking one question from 600 is a task.
Picking one from six is a choice.

**So: never show the library. Show six at a time, and a way to see six more.** The library can then
be as long as you like without anyone ever meeting its length. This is one screen, and it is the
screen someone opens every time an Edition starts, so it is worth getting right.

### What to put in it, judged on your own data rather than taste

Your live Edition has eleven questions, 143 answers and 521 hearts. Ranked by hearts per answer,
which is the closest thing to "did people enjoy reading this":

| question | answers | hearts each | how long people wrote |
|---|---|---|---|
| Are you a rider? Are you seeing someone? | 12 | **6.0** | 112 characters |
| What is a fun thing you did this summer? | 13 | 5.2 | 280 |
| Best thing you have eaten in the last month | 12 | 4.3 | 116 |
| Something new you did recently | 13 | 4.1 | 329 |
| Something creative you have recently done | 12 | 3.9 | 210 |
| A side quest you badly want to go on | 13 | 3.8 | 173 |
| Does your life look like you thought it would | 13 | 3.8 | 294 |
| Describe your month in 3 words | 11 | 3.3 | **33** |
| Who believes Sanan made this website? | 11 | 3.3 | 46 |
| How has coming from RV shaped your relationships | 10 | **2.9** | **618** |
| Songs you've had on repeat lately | 13 | **2.4** | 150 |

**Four things that says, and none of them is what you would guess.**

1. **The nosiest question won by a distance.** "Are you a rider? Are you seeing someone?" got six
   hearts an answer on 112 characters. People came to find out about each other, not to read essays.
2. **The most thoughtful question came last but one.** "How has coming from RV shaped your
   relationships" pulled the longest answers in the Edition, 618 characters, from the fewest people,
   and got the least love per answer of anything except the songs. A deep question is not a bad
   question — but it is a **one per Edition** question, not the shape of the whole thing.
3. **The songs question came last.** Thirteen people answered it and nobody much cared. Worth
   knowing, since the song previews are a whole build phase. The previews will make it better; they
   will not make it the best question on the page.
4. **The two shortest questions did fine.** "3 words" and the joke both landed mid-table on 33 and
   46 characters. That is your own point about one-liners, proved: the constraint lives in the
   wording and nobody needed the app to enforce it.

**So the library wants roughly this mix, per Edition of about eight questions:**

- **three or four that are nosy and fast** — what someone is up to, who they are seeing, what they
  ate, what they are annoyed by. These carry the Edition.
- **one that is properly deep.** One. It gets the long answers and it is the one people quote later.
- **one that is a joke about the group.** "Who believes Sanan made this website" is a poll wearing
  a text question's clothes, which is why F2 exists.
- **one answered with a photograph**, once the photo wall is drawn.
- **one that is a list or a line** — "three words", "a side quest" — because a page of paragraphs
  is a page nobody finishes.
- **one that only makes sense here.** The ghat road, assembly, your house, the banyan, the walk to
  the dining hall. These cannot be bought from a generic library and they are the reason this is
  not Letterloop.

### How to keep it short

Three rules, and together they mean about **sixty to eighty questions**, not six hundred:

1. **Write for this school, not for everybody.** A generic library is long because it is generic —
   it needs a hundred ways to say "how was your month" to cover a hundred kinds of group. You have
   one kind of group. Ten Rishi Valley questions beat a hundred neutral ones, and there are not six
   hundred of them to write.
2. **A Catch-up never sees a question it has already asked.** The library shrinks itself per group
   as it goes, so it stays fresh without ever growing. This is the rule that does the most work and
   it costs one filter.
3. **Let the good ones come back.** Track which questions got answered and hearted, exactly as
   above, and let the strong ones surface first for a group that has not had them. You have this
   data already; it is one query.

**Who writes them: you.** They are the voice of the thing, and this session can tell you what shape
they should be but not what they should say.

## 3. Time capsule, which is his

His words:

> "we should have maybe like a time capsule mode ... where it will release the addition only one
> year later. I don't know how we wire it up. Maybe something in settings. Say make this a time
> capsule. And we'd probably change the library a little bit, tweak it a little bit so that those
> questions are more relevant, maybe something's a little bit more personal, maybe some things what
> would you hope your life looks like in a year later or something like that."

**What a member sees.** The Catch-up's settings have a switch: this one is a time capsule. Everyone
writes as normal, the deadline passes, and instead of the Edition coming out, it is sealed. The
Catch-up's home says what it is sealed until. A year later it opens, everybody is told, and they
read what they wrote when they had forgotten writing it.

**Why it is better than the idea it replaces.** "This time last year" surfaces an old answer beside
a new one, which is pleasant. A time capsule changes what people write in the first place, because
they are writing to themselves in a year. That is a different and better thing, and it is the only
proposal on this page that changes the *content* rather than the container.

**The library tweak is half the feature, not a detail.** A sealed Edition wants questions that are
worth opening a year later: what you hope is different, what you are afraid of, what you would tell
yourself, where you think you will be living. A time capsule full of "best thing you ate this
month" is a wasted year.

### One thing a builder must know now, before it is too late

**Do not drop `CatchupEdition.publishAt` in build phase 11.**

Phase 11's cleanup is currently scheduled to drop that column, because the `preparing` hold it
served was deleted in phase 2. A time capsule is exactly a scheduled publish date, and it is the
same column. Dropping it and adding it back is two migrations against a live database for no
reason. Phase 11 should keep it and say why in the file.

This is the sort of thing that is free to notice today and expensive in November.

## 4. The photo wall, which was never optional

`photo-wall` has been a question kind since the feature was built and **has never been drawn**. It
is ¶16, it is a LOCKED decision (D10), and it comes before build phase 8 because its reading
surface can change a page that is already drawn.

The answering half is nearly free. The reading half is the whole question, and his rule is that it
must be *"modular and work with everything else"*.

**A grid is the wrong answer.** Twenty-four photographs in a grid is a contact sheet, and a contact
sheet is the one thing a magazine designer would never print. Three shapes worth drawing before
choosing:

- **A run.** One band the width of the column, photographs at their own shapes, moving sideways.
  Reads as a strip of film. Closest to what an answer's photographs already do.
- **A drift.** Photographs down the column at varying sizes, each with its contributor's bird
  beside it. Reads as a wall. Hardest to keep from looking ragged.
- **A stack.** One at a time, full column, contributor underneath, and you move through them. Reads
  as a slideshow. The only one of the three that survives two hundred photographs, which the
  pressure corpus already carries.

**Draw all three in one lab room and pick with him.**

## 5. Where the research came from

- [letterloop.co](https://www.letterloop.co/), read 2026-09-09: 600+ questions, up to 50 members,
  email delivery, photographs, members write their own questions.
- [Letterloop on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/letterloop/id6468623700): reactions
  and comments on replies and photographs, Spotify links, unlimited archives.
- [Remento](https://www.remento.co/journal/exploring-the-best-storyworth-alternatives-for-capturing-and-preserving-family-memories),
  [StoryWorth](https://welcome.storyworth.com/blog/remento---how-it-works-pricing-reviews-top-alternatives),
  [Heritage Whisper](https://heritagewhisper.com/alternatives/storyworth-alternatives),
  [Tell Mel](https://tellmel.ai/blog/storyworth-alternatives): the whole memory-capture category has
  moved to **voice** — recorded, telephoned, transcribed — on the shared diagnosis that typing is
  the barrier rather than willingness. Their product is the printed keepsake, which is track M.

**Parity with Letterloop, read against the live product.** Eleven rows, and one real gap.

| Letterloop | Us |
|---|---|

| Photographs in an answer | yes, and ours open in a viewer |

| Unlimited archive | yes |
| Pause, extend, reschedule | yes |
| Up to 50 people | ours is 100 |

**What was NOT measured, and it is a real limit.** Nobody signed up to Letterloop or opened one of
its published issues or its composer. The table above is read off marketing pages and a store
listing, which is the same limit `prior-art.md` §1 carries and F15 warns about. If the library or
the email issue becomes real work, look at the product first.
