### T2b-01 — One pasted link to a hostile page makes the server spin for minutes in the background, on every save of that answer and every read of that Edition, for ever
- Severity: High
- Confidence: certain on the mechanism (timed below); likely on the platform consequence (argued from Vercel's `after()` semantics, not measured on Vercel)
- Where:
  - `src/lib/link-preview-core.ts:379` — the attribute regex `/([a-zA-Z_:.-]+)\s*=\s*(?:"([^"]*)"|'([^']*)'|([^\s"'>]+))/g` run over every `<meta ...>` tag
  - `src/lib/link-preview-core.ts:377` — `for (const tag of head.matchAll(/<meta\b[^>]*>/gi))` (one tag may be the whole 512 KB)
  - `src/lib/link-preview-core.ts:389` — `/<title\b[^>]*>([\s\S]*?)<\/title\s*>/i.exec(head)` (lazy scan to the end for every unclosed `<title` opener)
  - `src/lib/link-preview-core.ts:373-374` — `headEnd === -1 ? html : html.slice(0, headEnd)`: a page with no `</head>` hands the whole buffer to the regexes
  - `src/lib/link-preview.ts:84` — `CAP = { html: 512 * 1024, ... }`; `:215-239` — the body is read up to the cap when `</head>` never arrives
  - `src/lib/link-preview.ts:329-331` — `parsePageMeta(decodeText(page), page.url)` inside `resolve()`
  - `src/lib/link-preview.ts:340-357` — `resolveAndStore`: the `linkPreview.upsert` (and therefore `failedAt`) is written only AFTER `resolve()` returns
  - `src/lib/link-preview-core.ts:435-438` — `needsResolve`: no row means "resolve it again"
  - `src/app/(main)/catchups/actions.ts:1966` — the save trigger: `scheduleLinkPreviews(findLinks(bodyValue)...)`
  - `src/lib/catchups-edition-view.ts:165-183` — the read trigger, on every render of the home and the reader
- Taxonomy: 6j (resource exhaustion), 6k (a link preview target that hangs), 6f (function duration), 6h (hostile input), 6a (quadratic loop)
- Expected: `link-preview.ts:8-22` promises "neither path can make a page wait, and neither can make one fail" and caps a page at 512 KB so "a news page's body can be a megabyte of nothing we use". The design assumes the cost of a page is bounded by its bytes.
- Actual: the cost is bounded by bytes SQUARED. A page whose `<head>` holds one `<meta ` tag of N filler characters ending in `>` makes the attribute regex backtrack O(N²): measured with the exact regex in Node, 20,000 chars = 1.15 s, 40,000 = 5.2 s, 80,000 = 17.4 s (four times the input, sixteen times the time). At the 512 KB the fetcher allows that extrapolates to roughly ten to twelve minutes of a single-threaded function. The `<title>` regex is the same shape: 20,000 unclosed `<title>` openers (140 KB) = 2.45 s, quadratic in the count. Both run inside `after()`, so the member who saved the answer sees "Saved" and nothing else; the function then burns until the platform kills it at its `maxDuration`. Because the kill lands before `resolveAndStore` writes its row, there is no `failedAt` and `needsResolve` answers true again on the NEXT page view of the Edition and the NEXT blur of the answer, so every reader of that Edition re-spawns the same background burn for as long as the answer exists. `inflight` (`link-preview.ts:338`) is per process and protects only an instance that survived the previous kill.
- Why: the attribute regex begins with an unbounded greedy class `[a-zA-Z_:.-]+` followed by an optional-whitespace `=`; on a run of legal characters with no `=` the engine tries every start position and, from each, every shorter length before failing (O(N²/2)). The title regex's `[\s\S]*?` scans to the end of the buffer once per `<title` occurrence when no `</title>` exists. Nothing between the socket and the regex bounds a single tag's length; `[^>]*` at `:377` happily matches half a megabyte. The attacker needs one static page on any free host and one answer body containing its address; the answering surface autosaves on every blur, and the Edition is read by every member after publication.
- Proof (read-only, no network): run the exact regexes from `link-preview-core.ts` on a crafted string. `node -e 'const R=/([a-zA-Z_:.-]+)\s*=\s*(?:"([^"]*)"|'"'"'([^'"'"']*)'"'"'|([^\s"'"'"'>]+))/g; const t="<meta "+"a".repeat(80000)+">"; const s=Date.now(); for(const m of t.matchAll(R)){}; console.log(Date.now()-s,"ms")'` prints ~17,000 ms (it printed 17,385 ms here). Or a `node --test` case that imports `parsePageMeta` and asserts a 512 KB single-tag head parses in under 200 ms; it fails today. Live: argue-only (it would require hosting the page and burning production compute).
- Fix direction: make `parsePageMeta` linear in its input. Bound the tag the attribute regex sees (`/<meta\b[^>]{0,4096}>/`), replace the backtracking attribute pattern with a bounded one (`[a-zA-Z_:.-]{1,64}` and an unquoted value class capped, or a hand-written scanner), and bound the title body (`[^<]{0,1024}` in place of `[\s\S]*?`). Independently, claim the URL BEFORE resolving: upsert a row with `failedAt: now` first and clear it on success, so a resolve the platform kills is retried once a day rather than on every view. Consider a per-page CPU deadline too (the whole `parsePageMeta` behind `setTimeout`-free code cannot be interrupted, which is why the bound must be in the regex).
- Gate: `src/lib/link-preview-core.test.mjs` gains a "hostile head" case: one 512 KB `<meta>` tag, and 30,000 unclosed `<title>` openers, each asserted to parse in under 200 ms and to yield no title. A second case pins that a row exists for a URL before its resolve completes (the claim-first write) if that half is taken.
- Known-related: none in the baseline. C-134 (open image optimiser as an amplifier) is the same family, billed compute triggered by a member, on a different door.

### T2b-02 — Link previews are unbounded work and unbounded storage that a member can grow at will, and nothing ever deletes a row or a thumbnail
- Severity: Medium
- Confidence: certain (every claim is a code fact; the rate of abuse is the only estimate)
- Where:
  - `src/app/(main)/catchups/actions.ts:1691-1978` — `submitEntry` has no rate limit at all (it is not in `LIMITS`, `src/lib/rate-limit.ts:41-186`; the only Catch-ups buckets are `catchups` for creation/nudge and `comments`)
  - `src/app/(main)/catchups/actions.ts:1966` — every save with a body schedules every link in it
  - `src/lib/link-preview.ts:90-91` — `MAX_PER_CALL = 16`, `CONCURRENCY = 4` (per call, not per member, not per hour)
  - `src/lib/link-preview.ts:286-300` — each ordinary link can cost a 512 KB page fetch plus a 5 MB image fetch, a sharp decode at the 100 MP ceiling (`src/lib/image.ts:17-20`) and one `putImage` into `link-previews/`
  - `src/lib/link-preview.ts:356` — the row is upserted for every distinct normalised URL; `link-preview-core.ts:136-145` keeps the path and every non-tracking query parameter, so `?n=1`, `?n=2`, ... are distinct rows and distinct objects
  - `src/lib/storage.ts:70-72` — `link-previews/` is deliberately outside `KNOWN_ROOTS`, so `keyForUrl`/`delImage` refuse to touch a thumbnail; there is no other deleter (grep for `link-previews` finds only the writer)
  - `src/lib/retention.ts` — no `linkPreview` anywhere; `src/lib/account-purge.ts` — none either
  - `prisma/schema.prisma:1313-1321` — no `createdAt`-style age column other than `fetchedAt`; the migration header (`2026-09-14-link-previews.sql`) says a row "outlives none of them in any way that matters"
- Taxonomy: 6j (unbounded tables, third-party fetch under load, per-member amplification), 6g (orphaned/never-reclaimed objects), 6i (no meter on a fanout)
- Expected: spec §10.1: "at most three redirects ... five seconds in all; bytes counted after decompression" — a bounded cost per link. The charter's seeded question 3 asks whether thumbnails are ever purged and whether one pasted link per answer can fill the bucket.
- Actual: per LINK the cost is bounded; per MEMBER it is not. A 6,000-character body holds roughly 300 distinct short URLs; each blur of that answer schedules 16 of them; a member (or a script with a member's session) can call `submitEntry` in a loop with fresh query strings and every call adds up to 16 server-side fetches of up to ~5.5 MB each, a decode, and a permanent object. Nothing is metered, nothing is aged out, and the thumbnails cannot be deleted by any code path in the repo even by hand through `delImage`. Today: 7 rows, 7 thumbnails. At 2,000 members writing normally this stays small; the finding is that the ceiling is the member's patience, not the code's.
- Why: quoted above. The `uploads` meter (`rate-limit.ts:94`, 40/hour) governs bytes the member sends, not bytes the member makes the server fetch; the design comment in `link-preview.ts:87-89` reasons about "an Edition with forty pasted links", i.e. good faith.
- Proof: read-only. `node scripts/dev/run-sql.mjs --inline "SELECT count(*) FROM \"LinkPreview\""` is 7 today; the absence of a deleter is the grep above. A throwaway-account proof would be: paste a body of 40 distinct `https://example.com/?n=<i>` links, blur, and watch `LinkPreview` grow by 16 per blur with `thumbUrl` objects that no code can remove.
- Fix direction: (1) meter the schedule, not the save: a `linkPreviews` bucket keyed on the member counting NEW urls handed to `scheduleLinkPreviews` (e.g. 30/hour), applied in `submitEntry` before scheduling; (2) cap distinct links per body at save time (e.g. 10) with a sentence; (3) give `LinkPreview` an owner-less lifecycle: a retention row that deletes rows whose `fetchedAt` is older than N months and whose URL no longer appears in any `CatchupEntry.body` (or simpler: on `deleteEntry`/purge, re-count references), deleting the thumbnail by KEY through `delImageByKey` (the root stays outside `KNOWN_ROOTS`; the retention path already holds keys); (4) an R2 lifecycle rule cannot be used on `link-previews/` while rows point at it, so (3) is the only reclaim path.
- Gate: `rate-limit` test asserting the new bucket exists and `submitEntry` calls it; a retention test that a stale unreferenced row and its object are removed; `security-regressions`-style test that `link-previews/` never joins `KNOWN_ROOTS` (keeps the C2 reasoning intact).
- Known-related: C-063 (staged objects never reclaimed) and C-064 (processed uploads orphan without reconciliation) are the sibling classes for `staging/` and `uploads/`; this is a third root with a third reason, and any reconciliation built for C-064 must include `link-previews/` and `audio/`.

### T2b-04 — The two-minute recording cap is a byte cap in disguise: a low-bitrate client can attach twenty minutes of audio that passes every check
- Severity: Medium (the owner's stated rule, "32 is 2 minutes", is not enforceable by anything the server does; needs a deliberate client, so arguably Low)
- Confidence: likely (arithmetic and code are certain; whether a real MediaRecorder honours `audioBitsPerSecond: 32000` for Opus is browser-dependent but Chrome does)
- Where:
  - `src/lib/voice-answer-rule.ts:34` — `VOICE_CEILING_BITS_PER_SECOND = 320_000`; `:53-55` — `voiceByteCap(seconds) = ceil(seconds * 320000 / 8) + 64000`; `:59` — `VOICE_MAX_BYTES = voiceByteCap(120)` = 4,864,000
  - `src/lib/voice-answer-rule.ts:43-52` — the docblock: "A file that claims two minutes and holds ten seconds passes, and all that costs is a wrong number on the player's rail" (the reverse direction is not mentioned)
  - `src/app/api/upload/audio/route.ts:56-68` — presign checks `seconds` in 1..120 and `bytes <= voiceByteCap(seconds)`
  - `src/app/api/upload/audio/finalize/route.ts:61-66` — finalize checks the HEAD size against `voiceByteCap(seconds)` and sniffs four magic bytes; nothing reads a duration
  - `src/app/(main)/catchups/actions.ts:1786-1788` — `submitEntry` re-checks only `validVoiceSeconds(seconds)` and ownership; the `seconds` it stores is the client's claim
- Taxonomy: 6h (boundary value: limit+1 in the dimension the server cannot see), 6g (client uploading different bytes than validated), L9 (comment tells half the truth)
- Expected: spec §16 phase 12 "audio up to two minutes"; `2026-09-14-voice-answers.sql` header "the two-minute cap is one constant in src/lib/voice-answer-rule.ts".
- Actual: Opus speech at 32 kbps is 240 KB a minute; 4.8 MB holds twenty minutes. A client that records at that bitrate, claims `seconds: 120` at presign and finalize, and sends the same to `submitEntry`, gets a published answer with a twenty-minute recording; the rail draws 2:00 until the `<audio>` element reads the real duration, the export copies it, and every reader who presses play gets twenty minutes. Storage is bounded (4.8 MB) so this is nuisance and a broken promise, not a cost.
- Why: the server "does not decode audio and cannot read a length out of Chrome's recordings" (`voice-answer-rule.ts:44-46`), so it bounds length by bytes at a generous ceiling bitrate; a bitrate ten times lower than the ceiling buys ten times the length.
- Proof: arithmetic (`voiceByteCap(120)` = 4,864,000; 4,864,000 × 8 / 32,000 = 1,216 s ≈ 20 min). Live proof needs a crafted client against a throwaway account and is not worth production bytes; argue-only.
- Fix direction: read the container's duration where it is cheap and present: MP4 `mvhd` (Safari), Ogg last-page granule position (Firefox), WebM `Duration` element or last Cluster/SimpleBlock timecode (Chrome writes no Duration, but the last Cluster's timecode is in the file's tail, a ranged GET away). Refuse or clamp when it exceeds 120 s, and store the READ duration rather than the claim. If that is judged too much for a lab-only feature, lower the ceiling bitrate to what the recorder actually asks for (64 kbps → 1 MB → ~4 min at 32 kbps) and say the residual in the comment.
- Gate: `voice-answer-rule.test.mjs` with a fixture header per container asserting the parsed duration; or, for the cheaper fix, a test pinning the ceiling constant to the recorder's requested bitrate.
- Known-related: none.

### T2b-08 — The export reads every photograph and recording through the public host with no timeout, the one thing TRAPS says a bulk reader must not do
- Severity: Low
