## The table

Every date computation that decides something a member or the owner sees, across the whole codebase, judged against four clocks: **Vercel (UTC, bom1)**, **the database session (UTC; columns `timestamp without time zone` holding UTC wall time)**, **the members (IST, +05:30, no DST; 42 of the ~180 members who visited in the last 30 days were in the US or the UK, 136 in India, per T7b-15)** and **the owner's laptop (Europe/London since 2026-09-17 22:13 — `/etc/localtime`; it was on IST before that, which is what L3-02's exports show)**. Three facts carry every row: (1) Prisma's `pg` adapter writes a `Date` with `getUTC*` and reads `timestamp` as `+00:00`, so the APP is zone-safe at the database boundary on any machine (T7b's fact 2; nothing in `src` opens a raw `pg` client — grep for `from "pg"`/`new Pool(` in `src` is empty); (2) raw `pg` in `scripts/` parses type 1114 with `new Date(y, m, d, h, …)` in the process's LOCAL zone (`node_modules/postgres-date/index.js:50`) and type 1082 (`date`) as local midnight (`:75`) — nothing sets a parser; (3) **measured cron times**: Vercel's `0 2 * * *` tick fires at **~02:53:35 UTC = 08:23 IST** every day for this project (four cron-written notification batches: 09-04 02:53:36, 09-06 02:53:35 — Batch of 2024's `publishedAt` 02:53:35.048 — 09-12 02:53:36, 09-18 02:53:36); GitHub's scheduler runs `backup.yml` (20:30 UTC) a median **2 h 12 m** late (min 0 h 16, p90 3 h 24, max 7 h 54, n=36), `retention.yml` (21:00 UTC) a median **1 h 54 m** late (min 0 h 13, p90 3 h 13, max 8 h 02, n=35), and `snapshot.yml` a median ~4 h 30 m late (T7b-05). No date library is installed; no `use cache`/`unstable_cache` exists, so no rendered date can be frozen by a cache (the one cached fetch, `wordle.ts`, is keyed by the IST date).

T7b's 42-row table for analytics, presence, messages, notifications and the snapshot (`work/reports/T7b.md`, "Date computations in T7b's territory") is folded in by reference as rows T7b-1…T7b-42 and not repeated; its ✗ rows are T7b-05 (snapshot "yesterday" captured hours into today), T7b-15 (deadlines named as a day that opens closed; rows 40-41) and the comment-only rows 10/13.

## Findings

### L3-01 — On a day the mail budget is spent, every member queued two or more days out is told their link "goes out tomorrow at 5:30 IST" (C-161 was fixed in the arithmetic and not in the sentence)
- Severity: Medium (reached exactly on the day it matters: the owner expects more than 100 signups on launch day (`email-queue.ts:35-36`); the first 75 confirmations of a UTC day go out at once and the next 75 are correctly told "tomorrow", so every signup from about the 151st of a day onward — position 75+ in the verify queue — reads a promise that fails the next morning, and at C-161's own 300-signup scenario that is half of them)
- Confidence: certain (scratch node run of the verbatim functions, below; the dialog and the banner share the one label function)
- Where: `src/lib/email-queue.ts:891-902` (`verifySendingAt`: `return nextBudgetResetAt(Math.floor(ahead / VERIFY_PER_DAY) + 1);` with `VERIFY_PER_DAY = 95 - 20 = 75` at `:876`), `:867-872` (`nextBudgetResetAt(daysForward)`), `:995-998` (`queued` state carries it: `sendingAt: await verifySendingAt(row)` at `:997`); `src/components/auth/verify-email-banner.tsx:51-66` (`sendTimeLabel`: `const sameDay = valleyDayKey(d) === valleyDayKey(now); return sameDay ? \`at ${time} IST\` : \`tomorrow at ${time} IST\`;` — two outcomes for any number of days), `:142-146` and `:195-200` (the banner: "Your link goes out {timeLabel || "tomorrow"}"); `src/components/auth/verify-email-dialog.tsx:116-117` (the resend button's sentence, same function); `src/app/(main)/layout.tsx:181` and `src/components/auth/email-actions.ts:114` (both paths hand the multi-day `sendingAt` through).
- Taxonomy: 6h dates (a relative day word computed for a date it cannot express) · 6j (the launch-day queue) · dedup: C-161 fixed incompletely
- Expected: C-161's own fix comment, `email-queue.ts:881-887`: "on a launch day with 300 signups the person at position 200 was told 'tomorrow' and waited three days ... A promise a member can watch fail is worse than a vaguer one that holds." The ETA was made queue-aware so the promise would hold.
- Actual: the ETA is right and the words are not. `sendTimeLabel` only knows "same valley day" and "tomorrow", so a send booked for the second, third or fourth UTC midnight reads "tomorrow at 5:30 IST" all the same. Worked (scratch run, verbatim functions, identical under `TZ=UTC` and `TZ=Asia/Kolkata`): now Thu 1 Oct 15:30 IST — 0 ahead → sends Fri 05:30, banner "tomorrow at 5:30 IST" (right); 75 ahead → sends **Sat** 05:30, banner "tomorrow at 5:30 IST"; 150 ahead → **Sun**, "tomorrow"; 225 ahead → **Mon**, "tomorrow". From 00:00-05:30 IST the two-day case happens to read right (the valley day has already turned) and three or more days never do. The member at position 200 of C-161's own scenario is told "tomorrow at 5:30 IST" and waits until Sunday, which is C-161's consequence reproduced word for word. Secondary: the rendered hour is "5:30" (en-GB 24-hour), not the "5:30 am" every comment quotes.
- Why: C-161's fix changed `verifySendingAt` to count the queue and left the formatter that phrases its result untouched; `mail-queue-rule.test.mjs:136-153` pins the arithmetic (`outboundEmail.count`, `createdAt: { lt:`, `VERIFY_PER_DAY`) and nothing pins the sentence a member reads.
- Proof: pure, already run — `sendTimeLabel(nextBudgetResetAt(now, floor(ahead/75)+1).toISOString(), now)` for ahead ∈ {0, 74, 75, 149, 150, 224, 225} prints "tomorrow at 5:30 IST" for every row at 15:30 IST. A test importing the label (after extracting it from the `"use client"` file, which `node:test` cannot load) with `now` injected would fail today.
- Fix direction: have `sendTimeLabel` phrase the valley-day distance it is given: `valleyDaysBetween(d, now)` → 0 "at 5:30 IST", 1 "tomorrow at 5:30 IST", 2+ "on Saturday at 5:30 IST" (`formatDayAndDate` voice, or the weekday alone inside a week); consider "5:30 am" (`hour12`) if the comments' phrasing is the intent. Keep the `useSyncExternalStore` client-only render (the day comparison still reads `now`).
- Gate: move `sendTimeLabel` into a pure module with an injectable `now` and pin, at 15:30 IST, that a send two valley days away does not contain "tomorrow"; keep the existing `mail-queue-rule` arithmetic pin.
- Known-related: C-161 (dispositioned fixed; this is its unfixed half), C-037 (the zone is right; this is the day count), T5's clean note on `sendTimeLabel` (it checked the one-day case across 18:30 UTC, which is correct).

### L3-02 — The "totally regeneratable" Catch-ups export records every timestamp shifted by the laptop's own UTC offset: all four exports on disk are 5 h 30 m early, so a rebuild would move every deadline and publication and put small-hours dates on the wrong day (bugs.md #6, live on the read side)
- Severity: Medium (latent data corruption in the one archive the owner asked to survive the rework; the nightly `pg_dump` is correct and is the real floor, which is what keeps this from High)
- Confidence: certain (measured: the four export files against the live rows cast `::text`)
- Where: `scripts/dev/export-catchups.mjs:81` (`new pg.Client(...)` with no `pg.types.setTypeParser(1114, ...)`), `:85` (`const iso = (d) => (d ? new Date(d).toISOString() : null);` over every raw-pg `timestamp without time zone` value, which node-pg has already parsed as LOCAL wall time), every `iso(...)` in the row mappers below it; `src/lib/catchups-export.ts:1-30` (the promise: "no member's words or photographs can be lost by a migration ... totally regeneratable", "Dates are ISO strings"); the same read-side shape, one-off and already applied: `scripts/dev/.anon-cleanup/apply.mjs:105,127,146,157` (the undo state records `createdAt.toISOString()` of raw-pg reads, so `--undo` would write the prior posts and the re-inserted comments back 5 h 30 m early; `redate.mjs` beside it knew the trap and reads `::text`).
- Taxonomy: 6h dates (a script that runs somewhere other than UTC) · bugs.md #6 made live · 6k (restore from the archive)
- Expected: `catchups-export.ts`'s contract — every field the app can render, rebuildable; `redate.mjs`'s own header states the rule this breaks ("node-postgres serialises a Date in the machine's LOCAL zone, which is how raw pg writes end up 5h30 off").
- Actual (proved): Catch-up `cmsepxoas000d04jr6xc30y3z` was created at 2026-08-04 13:53:23.524 UTC (`"createdAt"::text`); every export on disk says `2026-08-04T08:23:23.524Z` — the 2026-09-05, 09-07, 09-08 and 09-14 exports, shift −5.5 h in all four. Batch of 2024's Edition 1 closed answers at 2026-09-04 18:30:13 UTC (00:00 IST on **5 September**); the 09-14 export says `2026-09-04T13:00:13.663Z` (18:30 IST on **4 September**) — the deadline's valley date moved a day in the archive. Its publication (02:53 UTC 6 Sep) and `nextOpensAt` (02:53 UTC 6 Oct) are 21:23 UTC the day before in the file. The −5.5 h is the laptop's zone at export time: `/etc/localtime` on this Mac was re-pointed to Europe/London on 2026-09-17 22:13, and the repository's own commit stamps show the move (`git log --date=iso-strict`, 2026-09-15..20: 34 commits at `+05:30`, 6 at `+04:00`, then `+01:00`), so the September exports ran on IST; an export run today would be −1 h (BST) and one run in winter would be right.
