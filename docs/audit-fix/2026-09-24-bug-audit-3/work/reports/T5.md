# T5 — auth-email-tokens
Agent: T5 · Started: 2026-09-24 · Model: Fable 5.1 (claude-fable-5-1)
Charter: I own the sign-in and sign-up door and everything behind it: NextAuth v5 JWT sessions and their `credentialVersion` revocation, the credentials `authorize()` path, the signup action and form, the forgot/reset/verify/resend email actions and their `AuthToken` rows, the outbound mail queue (`OutboundEmail` + `QueueLease`, the 95/day Resend budget, the drain, folds, deferrals, the admin retry), the Resend webhook, the Turnstile / human-pass / rate-limit stack (Upstash, fails open), the `LoginAttempt` record, dev-login, the trivia and member-verification gates, email normalization everywhere an address is compared, and what `security-regressions.test.mjs` actually pins. Method: read every owned file end to end, walked each write path from form field to row, then tried to refute each hypothesis against the code, the installed library sources (`@auth/core`, `next-auth/react`, `@upstash/ratelimit`, `@upstash/redis`, `bcryptjs`, `@sentry/node-core`, Next's `after`) and read-only queries on the live database (222 members, 24 unconfirmed, 231 queue rows, 324 login attempts).

## Findings

### T5-01 — A member who mistyped their email at signup is stuck read-only for ever: nothing in the app lets them or the admin change the login address, and every recovery the app offers points at that impossible edit
- Severity: High
- Confidence: certain (mechanism read end to end; what would change my mind is a `User.email` writer I failed to find — the grep below covers `src/` outside `generated/` and `lab/`)
- Where:
  - `src/components/auth/actions.ts:155` — the ONE write of `User.email` in the application (`email: parsed.data.email` inside `prisma.user.create`).
  - `src/app/(main)/admin/people/actions.ts:94-96` — the admin editor refuses it on purpose: *"Deliberately NOT here: email (it is the login and the unique key, and changing it out from under somebody locks them out)"*.
  - `src/components/profile/profile-actions.ts` — only `displayEmail`/`showEmail` are writable (lines 265-330); `validators.ts:153-156` says *"editing it never changes the login email"*.
  - `src/app/(main)/admin/mail/actions.ts:13-15` — the Retry button's premise: *"Since the commonest cause is a mistyped address that has since been corrected on the person's record, 'try again' is the obvious next move"*.
  - `src/lib/email-queue.ts:584,596-602` — `render()` mints and addresses from `row.to`, the address frozen at enqueue; `email-queue.ts:323` sends to `row.to`. `retryMail` (`admin/mail/actions.ts:53-56`) writes only `RETRY_RESET` (`mail-policy.ts:135-142`), never `to`.
  - `src/components/auth/email-actions.ts:100-101` — the resend button's failure copy: *"That did not go through. Check the email address on your profile, then try again."*
  - `src/app/(auth)/verify-email/verify-client.tsx:44-47` and `reset-password/reset-client.tsx:46-49` — copy for a "stale" link: *"Your email address changed after this was sent"* — a state no member can reach.
  - `prisma/schema.prisma:596-599` (`AuthToken.sentTo`: *"Kept so a reset issued before someone changes their email cannot be redeemed afterwards"*) and `src/lib/auth-tokens.ts:302-306` — machinery for an email change that does not exist.
  - There is also no signed-in password change anywhere (`grep -ri changePassword\|updatePassword\|newPassword src` outside the reset flow finds nothing; the only `bcrypt.hash` callers are signup, `resetPassword` and `scripts/dev/set-password.mjs`), so the mailbox is the ONLY way to change a password too.
- Taxonomy: 6h identity states (unverified user), 6k (email provider bounce), comment lies, 6a dead end
- Expected: the trust model (docs/SECURITY.md table) says Stage 0 is a temporary state on the way to Stage 1; the admin spec §9.4 says the detail page "must cover exactly what has actually been fixed by hand"; the mail spec §9.8 says a failure gets "a retry".
- Actual: a member who typed `gmial.com` (or any address they cannot read) signs in fine and can never confirm. The banner nags on every page for ever; "Send it again" re-sends to the same wrong address; the admin's "Try again" on the bounced row re-sends to the same wrong address (`row.to` is never re-read from the person's row, and the person's row cannot be corrected anyway); the resend error tells them to fix it "on your profile" where the field is not editable; `requestPasswordReset` for the right address they meant answers `{ ok: true }` and mails nobody. The account can post nothing, upload nothing, see no contact details, and cannot even change its password (mailbox-only). The only exits are the owner running SQL by hand or the member creating a second account (which the unique check allows, leaving an orphan Stage-0 row in the batch group and the directory counts).
- Why: `User.email` has exactly one writer (`actions.ts:155`). The queue copies the address into `OutboundEmail.to` at enqueue (`email-queue.ts:490`), `render()` and `sendMail` use that copy (`584`, `323`), and `retryMail` resets counters only (`admin/mail/actions.ts:53-56`; `RETRY_RESET` at `mail-policy.ts:135-142` has no `to`). Even if an email edit existed, the retry would still target the stale copy, and a token minted for it would fail `readToken`'s `stale` check (`auth-tokens.ts:305-306`) against the corrected row. Live data today: 2 of the 24 unconfirmed members have a verify row bounced `MailboxFull` (recoverable by retry); the typo case is the same mechanics with no recovery. 22 more have a *delivered* verify mail they have not clicked — those are fine.
- Proof: read-only. (1) `grep -rn --include='*.ts' --include='*.tsx' --exclude-dir=generated --exclude-dir=lab -E "^\s+email: (parsed|input|data|next|new|normalized|trimmed|normalizeEmail|canonical|values|form)" src` → only `actions.ts:155` (plus LoginAttempt's own column). (2) `node scripts/dev/run-sql.mjs --inline "SELECT \"lastError\", attempts FROM \"OutboundEmail\" WHERE status='failed'"` → two `Bounced (MailboxFull)` rows. (3) With the throwaway account: sign up with an address you control but mistype the domain on purpose; observe there is no control anywhere (profile, settings, admin person page) to change it; press the admin Retry and watch the new row's `to` equal the typo. Argue-only for the "second account" consequence.
- Fix direction: (a) an email-change flow that the existing machinery was built for: member enters a new address → `emailVerified` cleared (or kept until the new one confirms), a verify mail to the NEW address, the `stale` check retires old links, and `credentialVersion` untouched (it is the same person); (b) the admin person page gets a "correct login email" verb that writes `User.email` (canonical form), and re-queues the failed verify mail; (c) `render()` (or `retryMail`) re-resolves `to` from the user row when `userId` is set, so a retry after a correction reaches the corrected address; (d) drop or fix the three copy lines that name an edit that cannot be made; (e) consider a signed-in password change (T6) so the mailbox is not the only credential path.
- Gate: a node test that some server action besides `registerUser` writes `User.email` (or, until then, that the three copy strings do not promise it); a `mail-policy` test that a retry of a row whose user's email differs re-targets the user's current email; `email-normalization-rule.test.mjs` already allowlists `sentToEmail`, extend it to the new writer.
- Known-related: dedup-baseline C-102 (retry zombie — fixed; this is the OTHER half of the same button), C-033 (blocked/no-password reset silently refused — same silent `{ ok: true }` shape hides the typo case too).

### T5-02 — A sign-in outage, a broken bot check or a missing mail key never reaches the owner: every alarm on the auth and mail path is a console line, `authorize` deliberately converts a database failure into a non-error, and Sentry captures neither
- Severity: High
- Confidence: likely (library facts confirmed against the installed sources; what would change my mind is a Sentry console-capture integration configured somewhere I did not look — I checked `src/instrumentation.ts`, `src/instrumentation-client.ts`, `next.config.ts` and the `@sentry/node-core` default integration list)
- Where:
  - `src/lib/auth.ts:311-315` — `catch (err) { if (err instanceof CredentialsSignin) throw err; console.error("[auth] sign-in could not be completed:", err); throw new LoginUnavailable(); }` — a DB timeout, pool exhaustion or any other failure at the door becomes a `CredentialsSignin`.
  - `node_modules/@auth/core/index.js:120-121,133-134` — `catch (e) { const error = e; logger.error(error); … if (error instanceof CredentialsSignin) params.set("code", error.code); … return Response.json({ url })` — the error is LOGGED (console) and answered as a normal redirect payload; nothing is thrown out of the route, so `onRequestError` (`src/instrumentation.ts:86`) never fires.
  - `src/instrumentation.ts:11-17` — the promise: *"Server errors -- 500s, failed Server Actions, database errors, the whole class that actually bit us -- ARE caught."* `src/instrumentation.ts:42-72` — no `captureConsoleIntegration`; `node_modules/@sentry/node-core/build/cjs/sdk/index.js:37` — the default `consoleIntegration()` records console output as BREADCRUMBS only (attached to a later event, never an event itself); `captureConsoleIntegration` lives in `@sentry/core/build/cjs/integrations/captureconsole.js` and is not enabled.
  - `src/lib/report-error.ts:13-19` — the repo's own diagnosis: *"`console.error` on Vercel is a line in a log nobody reads, and Sentry … only ever sees what is THROWN."* The helper exists; the sites below do not use it.
  - Sites in my territory whose only witness is `console.*`: `src/lib/auth.ts:181-183` (the `login-unverified` warn — seeded question 4), `:291` (delete-cancel notification failed), `:313`; `src/lib/turnstile.ts:153` (secret unset in production: *"bot checks are OFF"*), `:180` (siteverify refused), `:195-197` (wrong-host refusal), `:202` (siteverify unreachable, failing open); `src/lib/email-queue.ts:117-121` and `:133-137` (RESEND_API_KEY missing — *"queued mail cannot send"*), `:226-230` (lost claim / possible double send), `:767-770` (reclaimed rows); `src/lib/email.ts:193,222,227,241`; `src/components/auth/actions.ts:215` (verification email failed at signup); `src/components/auth/email-actions.ts:274` (`[reset] enqueue failed` — *"this log is the only witness"*); `src/lib/roster.ts:101`; `src/app/api/resend/webhook/route.ts:29,42,80,178,184`; `src/app/(main)/layout.tsx:120`.
  - Two documents state the opposite: `docs/SECURITY.md:268-269` — *"The refusal logs at `console.error`, so if that reasoning is ever wrong it appears in Sentry instead of quietly locking members out."* and `src/lib/turnstile.ts:188-194` — *"error, not warn: this is a refusal at the auth door, so it belongs in Sentry."* Neither is true.
- Taxonomy: 6a swallowed exceptions in telemetry, silent-failure-hunter, comment lies, 6k (DB down / Turnstile unreachable / mail provider down)
- Expected: the smoke alarm fires for "the whole class that actually bit us"; a wrong-host refusal or a silent lockout "appears in Sentry".
- Actual: sign-in can be down for every member (pool exhausted, DB paused, Supavisor hiccup) with each attempt printing "We could not reach the site just now" — and the owner learns of it only if he tries to sign in himself or reads Vercel's runtime log within its short retention. Turnstile misconfiguration (110200 storms, a stripped secret) and a stripped `RESEND_API_KEY` (queue grows, nobody gets a confirmation) are equally invisible. Bounces and spam complaints — the sender-reputation signals the webhook comment calls the most damaging — go to the same log.
- Why: `LoginUnavailable` is a `CredentialsSignin` by design (M18 needed a clean code for the form), and `@auth/core` treats every `CredentialsSignin` as an expected refusal: `logger.error` + a redirect payload, never a throw. Sentry's only inputs here are thrown errors reaching `captureRequestError` and explicit `captureException` calls (`reportSwallowed`). Console output becomes breadcrumbs. So the design that fixed M18 (honest copy) removed the throw that would have alerted anyone, and nothing replaced it.
- Proof: argue-only against the live site (no way to take the DB down). Read-only confirmation: `grep -rn captureConsole src next.config.ts` → nothing; `sed -n 30,40p node_modules/@sentry/node-core/build/cjs/sdk/index.js` → `consoleIntegration()` in the defaults, no `captureConsoleIntegration`; `sed -n 118,136p node_modules/@auth/core/index.js`. A safe local repro: run the dev server with `DATABASE_URL` pointed at a closed port, attempt a sign-in with the throwaway account, observe the form's "unavailable" sentence and (with `SENTRY_DEV=1`) that no Sentry event is emitted.
- Fix direction: at `auth.ts:313` call `reportSwallowed("auth", err, { step: "authorize" })` before throwing `LoginUnavailable` (it is the one place the outage is known); do the same at the `turnstile.ts` secret-missing / wrong-host / unreachable branches (throttled like `rate-limit.ts` does), the `email-queue.ts` key-missing and lost-claim/reclaim lines, `actions.ts:215`, `email-actions.ts:274` and the webhook's bounce/complaint lines (or write those two to the admin worklist). Alternatively enable `captureConsoleIntegration({ levels: ["error"] })` server-side — but then read T5-10 first, because the auth logger already prints `[auth][error] CredentialsSignin` for every wrong password and that would spend the quota on noise. Correct the two documents.
- Gate: a `-rule` test that every `console.error` in `src/lib/auth.ts`, `turnstile.ts`, `email-queue.ts`, `email.ts`, `email-actions.ts`, `actions.ts`, `roster.ts` and the resend webhook is within N lines of a `reportSwallowed(`/`captureException(` (the existing `test-kit` decomment/balancedBody helpers make this a short file); a doc test that `docs/SECURITY.md` does not claim console output reaches Sentry.
- Known-related: C-149 (advanceEdition bypassing the M09 reporter — same class, fixed there, not here); M09/`report-error.ts` is the mechanism, unused on these paths.

### T5-03 — When Upstash is unreachable the sign-in door does not fail open, it stalls: `hasBudget` bypasses the 3-second race and the Redis client retries five times with backoff, so every attempt waits at least ~4 seconds and, against a hanging endpoint, until the platform kills the function
- Severity: High
- Confidence: likely (argued from the installed library sources; a live measurement is the proof plan below)
- Where:
  - `src/lib/rate-limit.ts:15-20` — the contract: *"Posture on failure: OPEN … A rate limiter that can take down sign-in when Redis blinks would be a worse availability bug than the abuse it prevents."*
  - `src/lib/rate-limit.ts:224-226` — `timeout: 3000` with the comment *"If Redis does not answer promptly, the request proceeds (fail-open) rather than holding sign-in hostage."*
  - `src/lib/rate-limit.ts:286-297` — `hasBudget` calls `limiter.getRemaining(key)`; `:301-310` `consume` calls `limiter.limit(key)`.
  - `node_modules/@upstash/ratelimit/dist/index.js:821-835` — only `limit()` races the response against `applyTimeout` (`:928-943`); `:893-896` — `getRemaining = async (identifier) => this.limiter().getRemaining(this.ctx, pattern)` with no timeout at all.
  - `src/lib/rate-limit.ts:207` — `new Redis({ url, token })`: no `signal`, no `retry` override. `node_modules/@upstash/redis/nodejs.js:150-154` — defaults `attempts: 5`, `backoff: Math.exp(retryCount) * 50` ms; `:190-216` — the loop retries only fetch REJECTIONS (network failures), sleeping 50 + 136 + 369 + 1004 + 2730 ≈ 4.3 s across six connection attempts; a non-2xx (401 bad credential, 5xx) is thrown immediately without retry.
  - `src/lib/auth.ts:104-107` — `Promise.all([hasBudget("login-ip"), hasBudget("login-account")])` at the top of every sign-in; `:173` — a third `hasBudget("login-unverified")` for tokenless attempts; `src/components/settings/actions.ts:198` — `hasBudget("reauth")` in front of account deletion.
- Taxonomy: 6k (third party down), 6a resource/timeouts, comment lies, 6j third-party quotas
- Expected: Upstash blinking costs nothing; sign-in proceeds unmetered.
- Actual: with Upstash's host unreachable (DNS failure, connection refused, an outage that drops packets), every sign-in first waits for six connection attempts plus ~4.3 s of backoff — twice in parallel — before `hasBudget` fails open; if the connections hang instead of failing fast, each attempt waits for the OS/undici connect timeout per try (tens of seconds), the NextAuth route exceeds the function budget, and the browser gets a non-JSON 504 that `signIn()` cannot parse → the form shows "Something went wrong. Please try again." for the whole outage: the lockout the header comment says cannot happen. Signup, reset request and trivia are fine (they use `rateLimit()` → `limit()` → the 3 s race). A wrong or rotated Upstash token is NOT this bug: it 401s instantly and fails open (see T5-10 for what it does to Sentry).
- Why: the 3 s race is a property of `Ratelimit.limit()` alone. `getRemaining()` goes straight to the Lua script through the Redis HTTP client, whose default retry policy is meant for idempotent commands and whose only cancellation is a caller-supplied `signal` — which `rate-limit.ts` does not supply. `hasBudget`'s own `try/catch` only fires after the client gives up.
- Proof: a throwaway Node script under `/tmp` (deleted in the same command), no app involved: `new Ratelimit({ redis: new Redis({ url: "https://10.255.255.1", token: "x" }), limiter: Ratelimit.slidingWindow(5, "1 h"), timeout: 3000 })`, then time `await limiter.limit("k")` (expect ≈3 s, `reason: "timeout"`) against `await limiter.getRemaining("k")` (expect the retry sequence: several seconds to a minute depending on how the non-routable address fails on the Mac). Read-only, touches nothing shared.
- Fix direction: give the Redis client a per-request deadline and no retries on the door: `new Redis({ url, token, retry: { retries: 1, backoff: () => 0 }, signal: () => AbortSignal.timeout(3000) })` (the client accepts a signal FUNCTION so each request gets a fresh one — `nodejs.js:165-174`), and/or race `getRemaining` in `hasBudget` exactly as `limit` is raced. Keep `reportLimiterFailure`. Consider whether the door's read-then-spend shape is worth its two extra round trips at all (see T5-04).
- Gate: a unit test on `hasBudget` with an injected limiter whose `getRemaining` never resolves, asserting it returns `true` within the deadline; a comment fix at `rate-limit.ts:224-226` naming which call the timeout covers.
- Known-related: C-154 (limiter failures now reported — this is the OTHER half: the report happens only after the stall).

### T5-04 — The `login-unverified` allowance is not atomic although its comment says a racing caller "cannot outrun its own meter": N simultaneous tokenless sign-ins all pass; and the refusal it eventually gives carries the wrong advice
- Severity: Medium
- Confidence: certain (two separate Upstash calls; no compare-and-set)
- Where: `src/lib/auth.ts:173-180` — `const spare = await hasBudget("login-unverified", ip); // Spent before the verdict is acted on, so a caller that races many attempts through at once cannot outrun its own meter. await consume("login-unverified", ip); if (!spare) { … throw new BotCheckFailed(); }`; `src/lib/rate-limit.ts:286-297` (`getRemaining`, a read) and `:301-310` (`limit`, the increment); `src/lib/rate-limit.ts:47-70` (the meter's own comment: *"five an hour per IP makes the tokenless door useless to it"*). The same read-then-spend shape guards `login-ip`/`login-account` (`auth.ts:104-111` read, `:189-190` spend after bcrypt) and `reauth` (`settings/actions.ts:198-203`), where it is a documented design choice (successes must not count) rather than a claim of atomicity.
- Taxonomy: 6c(1)/6i races (check-then-act), 6e rate limiting on authorize, comment lies
- Expected: at most five tokenless attempts an hour from one address, "spent whether it succeeds or fails".
- Actual: a burst of N concurrent tokenless requests from one IP all read `remaining = 5`, all pass, all then increment; the sixth SEQUENTIAL request is the first refused. A stuffing run that fires 50 requests at once gets 50 bcrypt-checked guesses on the unverified door, then the ordinary meters take over (which have the same burst window: `login-account`'s "stopped by the tenth guess no matter how many IPs it rotates", `rate-limit.ts:42-46`, holds only for sequential guesses). Stability cost: 50 concurrent bcrypt cost-12 compares (~250 ms CPU each) on the auth function per burst.
- Second half: when the allowance IS spent, the refusal is `BotCheckFailed` → code `bot-check` → the form prints `BOT_CHECK_FAILED` (`login-client.tsx:177-183`, `bot-check-message.ts:10-11`): *"We couldn't confirm you're human. Refresh the page and try once more."* — for a browser the code's own comments say cannot produce a token, refreshing is exactly the advice `turnstile-widget.tsx:31-36` calls "the bug". The sixth colleague signing in from an office network that blocks `challenges.cloudflare.com` gets a loop with a friendly voice for the rest of the hour.
- Proof: read-only reasoning is sufficient for the race; live: 10 parallel `curl` POSTs to `/api/auth/callback/credentials` from one machine with no `turnstileToken` (throwaway account, wrong password so nothing signs in) and then `LoginAttempt` rows: expect >5 rows with `reason='wrong-password'` before any `bot-check`, in the same second. NOTE: only with the orchestrator's explicit go-ahead — it POSTs to an auth route, which my brief forbids me.
- Fix direction: this meter is spent on success and failure alike, so the two-step buys nothing: replace lines 173-180 with `const allowed = await rateLimit("login-unverified", ip); if (!allowed.ok) { …throw }` (one atomic `limit()`, already raced at 3 s). Give the spent-allowance refusal its own `CredentialsSignin` code (e.g. `unverified-budget`) and a sentence that says "try again in an hour, or from a network where the check can load". For the failure-only meters, either accept the burst window explicitly in the comment or move to spend-then-refund (a Redis `DECR` on success), and correct `rate-limit.ts:42-46`.
- Gate: `security-regressions.test.mjs` shape pin: `auth.ts` must not contain `hasBudget("login-unverified"`; a `bot-check-message`/login-client test that the spent-allowance code maps to its own sentence.
- Known-related: none in the baseline (the 2026-09-11 change post-dates audit 2).

### T5-05 — Take more than about half an hour on the signup form and it dead-ends: the trivia pass expires, every submit says "Please answer the entry question before signing up", and the question is no longer on screen
- Severity: Medium
- Confidence: certain (cookie TTLs and the step machine read end to end)
- Where: `src/components/auth/trivia-actions.ts:179` (`TOKEN_TTL_MS = 30 * 60 * 1000`), `:269-281` (`rv_trivia_id` set ONLY when missing, `maxAge` 30 min from the FIRST attempt), `:311-318` (`rv_trivia_pass`, 30 min from the pass), `:323-332` (`hasPassedTrivia` needs both cookies AND `stampValid` within 30 min); `src/components/auth/actions.ts:20-25` (`registerUser` refuses with *"Please answer the entry question before signing up."*); `src/app/(auth)/signup/signup-client.tsx:21,29,163-204` (`step` goes `"trivia" → "register"` and nothing ever sets it back); `src/components/auth/signup-form.tsx:416-424` (the error is printed under the form and the token reset; the form stays).
- Taxonomy: 6h dates/time windows, 6a dead end, 6k session-expires-mid-form
- Expected: the gate proves a human once; the form can be filled at a person's pace (the consent line opens three documents "in a new tab so the half-filled form is never lost", `signup-form.tsx:660-664`).
- Actual: the window is 30 minutes from the FIRST trivia attempt (the id cookie), not from passing — a member who gets one wrong, swaps a question, then passes at minute 9 has 21 minutes for nine fields, the batch explainer and three policy documents. Past it, "Join" answers with a sentence about a question that is not on the page; the only way out is a reload, which resets to the trivia step and discards everything typed. An older alumnus on a phone, reading the privacy policy the consent line asks them to read, is the person this hits.
- Why: `hasPassedTrivia` is the server-side truth and both cookies carry `maxAge: TOKEN_TTL_MS / 1000` (`:278`, `:316`); the browser deletes them at expiry; the client has no transition from `register` back to `trivia` and no handler for that specific error string.
- Proof: with the throwaway account, pass the gate, wait 31 minutes (or, read-only against a local dev server: pass the gate, delete the `rv_trivia_pass` cookie in devtools), submit → the sentence appears, no question visible, no way forward except reload.
- Fix direction: on that specific refusal, flip `step` back to `"trivia"` while KEEPING the form's values (lift the field state or keep the form mounted hidden), and say so ("the check timed out; answer it once more and your details are still here"); refresh the id cookie's `maxAge` when the pass is minted so both expire together; consider a longer window (2 h) — the pass is bound to the browser id and buys a bot nothing beyond one signup attempt on a rate-limited action.
- Gate: a test on the signup client's reducer/step logic (extract the "gate error → step" rule into a pure module) asserting the gate-error string routes back to the trivia step with fields intact.
- Known-related: M7 (the gate was moved server-side; this is its new failure mode).

### T5-06 — On the day the absolute 90-day session lapses, a member mid-form is told to "check your connection": the expired cookie turns every save into `ACTION_FAILED`, and a reload then loses what they typed
- Severity: Medium
- Confidence: likely (mechanism certain up to the exact client exception; the sentence shown is certain because `callAction` maps every rejection to it)
- Where: `src/lib/auth.ts:319-349` and `src/lib/session-revocation.ts:26-47` (ABSOLUTE ninety days; *"one forced re-login roughly three months after they signed in, and a launch cohort hitting it together on the same day"*); `src/app/api/dev-login/route.ts:135-141` (the cookie carries `expires`, so the browser deletes it on the day); `src/proxy.ts:173-175,296-320` (no session cookie → redirect to `/login?next=…` for everything not public, including a server-action POST); `src/lib/call-action.ts:5-8,23,43-58` (*"the session expires and the proxy answers the action POST with a redirect to /login"* → the rejection becomes `{ error: "That did not go through. Check your connection and try again." }`); `src/app/(main)/layout.tsx:26-38` (page navigations do get `next` back).
- Taxonomy: 6e maxAge, 6k session expires mid-form, 6h identity states (account expired mid-session), 6a error copy
- Expected: a lapse is "recoverable, with no data loss" (`auth.ts:332`).
- Actual: a member writing a letter, a Catch-up answer or a comment when the cookie's `expires` passes sees every save answered with a connection complaint. They check wifi, retry, same sentence. Nothing tells them to sign in; when they eventually reload, the layout redirects to `/login?next=…` and the in-page draft is gone unless that surface persists locally (T1: `use-letter-persistence.ts` mentions `credentialVersion` at `:322` — the orchestrator should confirm it also covers the expired-cookie path; Catch-up answers and upload finalize are T2b/T4a's).
