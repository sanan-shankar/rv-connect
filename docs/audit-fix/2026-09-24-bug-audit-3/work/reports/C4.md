### C4-01 — One anonymous script, one call every six seconds, takes over the new trivia room from its first hour and fills the database in about seven weeks: the hourly cap bounds the rate, not the size, and the log keeps a year
- Severity: Medium (the room the owner asked for on 2026-10-23 is blinded at once and silently; the database consequence is the L5-02 one — read-only for everyone — but slow, and the 350 MB alarm from `e29823cb` fires about three weeks before it)
- Confidence: certain on the mechanism and the arithmetic (quoted below, row sizes measured read-only); what would change my mind: an edge rule (Vercel WAF) in front of server-action POSTs to `/signup`, which nothing in the repo shows.
- Where:
  - `src/components/auth/trivia-actions.ts:55-77` — `getTriviaQuestion(excludeId?, said?)`, a public server action, NO meter of any kind ("No cost and no limit on swapping", `:45-47`), writes `dontknow` (with up to 200 characters of `said`) + `shown` on every call via `recordTrivia`
  - `src/components/auth/trivia-actions.ts:100-108` — `checkTrivia`: when the per-IP `trivia` meter has ALREADY refused, it still writes a `limited` row carrying the typed answer (`recordTrivia(browserId, [{ kind: "limited", questionId: q.id, answer: typed }])`), and the `addedAnswers` read at `:101-104` runs on the refused call too. So the per-IP limit stops nobody's writes.
  - `src/lib/trivia-log.ts:37-50` — the cap and its promise: "without a ceiling a script could write rows as fast as it could call the action, and a full 500 MB database turns read-only for everyone ... once the hour holds the cap, every later write is dropped"; `TRIVIA_ROWS_PER_HOUR = 600` site-wide; `:53` `ANSWER_MAX = 200`; `:126-141` the count-then-insert
  - `src/lib/retention.ts:83` — `gateLog: 365`; `:140` the sweep step
  - the same promise repeated: `docs/spec/admin.md:699` ("capped site-wide at `TRIVIA_ROWS_PER_HOUR` so a bot cannot fill the database"), `docs/SECURITY.md:105-109`, `src/lib/admin-trivia.ts:20-24` ("the hourly write cap means even a flood year stays near [30k rows]")
  - `src/lib/admin-trivia.ts:24,43-47` — `ROW_CEILING = 30_000`, newest first
- Taxonomy: 6j (unbounded tables vs the retention sweep; anonymous writers), 6k, comment-that-lies, 6c (unmetered public server action)
- Expected: the four places above promise the cap makes the table safe from a bot, and the room is the owner's view of real newcomers ("I want to know what people are typing in", spec §9.11).
- Actual:
  1. **The room.** The cap is first-come, site-wide. A script spending the 600 slots in the first minutes of each hour leaves nothing for the real visitors who arrive after; their rows are dropped without a trace, and the room's door funnel, "answers worth a look" and per-question verdicts are computed from the script's rows. A script sending no `rv_trivia_id` gets a fresh one per call (`trivia-log.ts:73-88`), so every call is a new "person" at the door. This starts in the first hour and is invisible: no row is written to say rows were dropped.
  2. **The database.** The cap bounds the RATE; the year's retention makes its steady state 600 × 24 × 365 = **5.26 million rows**. Measured read-only: a `limited`/`dontknow` row with a 200-character answer is 344 B of heap tuple (`pg_column_size(ROW(...))` with the table's column shapes), ~485 B with its line pointer and the three indexes (pkey on a 25-char cuid, `createdAt`, `browserId` on a 36-char uuid) — so the cap's own steady state is ~2.5 GB, five times the plan. At the cap the table grows ~290 KB an hour, ~7 MB a day. Today: `pg_database_size` = 128.5 MB (2026-10-24). The snapshot alarm (`scripts/ops/snapshot.mjs:80`, 350 MB of Postgres's own count) fires after ~32 days; Supabase's meter reads ~20 MB above Postgres's (`snapshot.mjs:181-182`), so read-only arrives at ~480 MB of Postgres's count, ~50 days in. The attacker's cost: one `checkTrivia` call every 6 s (one 485 B `limited` row each, after the first eight) or one `getTriviaQuestion(validId, "<200 chars>")` every 12 s (two rows). No session, no Turnstile, no Upstash spend on the swap path.
  3. **The room again, under that flood.** 14,400 rows a day against `ROW_CEILING = 30_000` newest-first: the "30 days" and "Everything" views silently show only the last ~50 hours (the `capped` flag is returned; whether the room says so is a T7b/UI question).
  4. Real peak traffic hits the same wall: the comment sizes the cap at "a hundred of them ... ~5 rows each is 500"; an hour with 120+ newcomers at the Centenary meet (the comment's own ~1,000-person event) loses rows on the one day the owner most wants them.
- Why: the cap copies `login-attempt.ts`'s `FAILURE_ROWS_PER_HOUR` shape (`e29823cb`, the L5-02 fix) — a per-hour ceiling with no per-source meter — onto a door that is FAR easier to reach (no CSRF token, no NextAuth route, no Upstash on the swap path) and with five times the hourly allowance, under the same 365-day retention. Neither the cap nor the retention was sized against the other.
- Proof: read-only, already done: `SELECT pg_database_size(current_database())` = 128,543,891; `SELECT pg_column_size(ROW('<25-char id>'::text,'<uuid>'::text,'banyan-age'::text,'limited'::text,repeat('x',200)::text,NULL::text,NULL::text,'desktop'::text,'IN'::text,NULL::text,now()::timestamp(3)))` = 344 (the `shown` shape = 136); live `TriviaEvent` = 3 rows, avg 157 B. A live flood must never be run against production; on a local server with `PRESENCE_IN_DEV=1` (which turns `statsWritesEnabled()` on, `stats-exclusion.ts:46-48`) and a throwaway database, N POSTs of the `getTriviaQuestion` action then `SELECT count(*) FROM "TriviaEvent" WHERE "createdAt" > now() - interval '1 hour'` = min(2N, 600), and every later real visit in that hour writes nothing.
- Fix direction: (1) meter the WRITER per source, not just the site: before `recordTrivia`, spend a per-IP allowance (the existing `rateLimit` with a new `triviaLog` bucket, generous: ~60 rows an hour per IP) and drop the row (not the answer to the visitor) when it is spent; (2) do not write a `limited` row with text on every refused call — at most one per IP per window ("this address was turned away N times" belongs in a counter, the L5-02 fix direction (1)); (3) size the retention to the cap or the cap to the retention: e.g. keep raw `shown`/`skipped` rows 90 days (they only feed counts) and the worded rows a year, or make the ceiling a TOTAL ("no inserts while the table holds more than X rows") rather than per hour; (4) correct the four promises (`trivia-log.ts:37-49`, spec §9.11, SECURITY.md, `admin-trivia.ts:20-23`); (5) the WAF rule L5-02 recommended covers this door too if it matches server-action POSTs to `/signup`.
- Gate: extend `src/lib/trivia-log-rule.test.mjs`: assert a per-source meter precedes `recordTrivia` in `getTriviaQuestion`, that `checkTrivia`'s refused branch records no answer text (or records through the meter), and an arithmetic pin that `TRIVIA_ROWS_PER_HOUR × 24 × KEEP_DAYS.gateLog × 485 B` stays under a stated budget (e.g. 100 MB).
- Known-related: L5-02 (the LoginAttempt flood; its verified-clean line "signup, reset and trivia refuse before they write ... `trivia-actions.ts:260-262` return on `rateLimit` before any query" is FALSE at HEAD since `42ec6fe7`: the refused trivia call now reads `TriviaAnswer` and writes a row); C2's L4-02 extension (the same writers are production-silent, so a broken write and a flooded hour look alike); L8's lead (`trivia-log.ts:40` "repeats the 500 MB fact and writes a row per unauthenticated attempt"), which this answers. Not C-163/C-164 (Visit, authenticated).

- Expected: the move's comment (`:123-132`) — "A confirmation still WAITING goes to the new address instead"; the fold's (`email-queue.ts:474-479`) — never answer "queued" while nothing went to the new address.
- Actual: reader selects the row (`to` = old) → the move's transaction re-addresses it → the claim succeeds (status is still `queued`) → the token is minted with `sentTo` = old (so its link answers "stale"), the mail goes to the old (bounced) address, and the row is stamped `sent` with `to` = new. `confirmationStillWaiting` now sees a SENT row to the new address → gate shut; the banner says "We sent a link to n***@new". - Known-related: L11-03 (an accepted-but-unrecorded send goes twice — a different window on the same claim); none in the baseline.
